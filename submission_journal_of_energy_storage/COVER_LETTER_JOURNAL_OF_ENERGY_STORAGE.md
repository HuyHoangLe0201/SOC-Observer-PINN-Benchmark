# Cover Letter - Journal of Energy Storage (Elsevier)

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled **"Comparative Benchmark of State-of-Charge Observers and Physics-Informed Neural Networks Across Battery Aging and Thermal Regimes"** for consideration in *Journal of Energy Storage*.

In battery state-of-charge (SOC) estimation, practitioners lack a principled basis for the most fundamental design decision: should engineering effort focus on classical model-based observers, adaptive covariance tuning, deep sequence architectures, or physics-informed hybrid estimation? This paper provides quantitative diagnostics and a unified dual-tier benchmark to answer that question before embedded deployment begins, evaluating all estimators under strictly blind fresh room-temperature priors without oracle health leakage. We develop a comparative and physics-informed estimation framework that yields three practical outputs: (1) a scalar aging-sensitivity diagnostic, specifically the Aging Sensitivity Factor (ASF), revealing that fixed-parameter Extended Kalman Filters (EKF) suffer a $55.9$-fold root-mean-square error (RMSE) surge ($0.067\%$ to $3.745\%$) from $100\%$ down to $80\%$ state-of-health (SOH) as charge-transfer impedance widens, whereas online parameter-tracking Dual EKF (DEKF) cuts aged error to $2.073\%$; (2) an analytical observability and dynamic hysteresis bias-cancellation formulation for flat-plateau $\text{LiFePO}_4$ (LFP) chemistry (strictly positive-definite $30\;\text{s}$ linear time-varying observability Gramians, $\lambda_{\min} \ge 1.09 \times 10^{-7}$ on UDDS), cutting fresh LFP tracking error nearly eightfold ($0.401\%$ to $0.049\%\text{--}0.051\%$) and scaling to distributed 8S series battery packs ($0.544\%$ weakest-cell RMSE); and (3) a hybrid Physics-Informed Dual Kalman Filter (PIDKF) pairing online DEKF tracking ($\hat{R}_{0,k}, 1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}$) with a $257$-parameter Butler-Volmer-passivity neural compensator protected via exogenous sensitivity decoupling and implemented as static zero-heap ANSI-C99 reference kernels ($17.74\;\upmu\text{s}$ per step on a $168\;\text{MHz}$ ARM Cortex-M4F, $4.28\;\upmu\text{s}$ on a $480\;\text{MHz}$ Cortex-M7, $364\;\text{B}$ RAM, $7.25\;\text{KB}$ Flash). Validation across Tier 1 public cycler datasets (XJTU Batch-5 NMC532 Random Walk cells, NASA Ames PCoE B0005/B0007/B0030/B0055/B0056 aging and $4^\circ\text{C}\text{--}43^\circ\text{C}$ thermal records, and NASA B0006 electrochemical impedance spectroscopy showing $R_{\text{ct}}$ broadening from $22.37$ to $38.89\;\text{m}\Omega$) and Tier 2 calibrated electro-thermal-aging co-simulation across ten phases confirms that PIDKF uniquely maintains $80\%$ SOH RMSE below $1\%$ ($0.928 \pm 0.027\%$), attains $0.554 \pm 0.018\%$ (NMC) and $0.593 \pm 0.048\%$ (LFP) on unseen aged $85\%$ schedules, achieves $1.425\%\text{--}2.750\%$ on held-out XJTU cells and $0.636\%\text{--}1.715\%$ on NASA B0007, and reduces $4^\circ\text{C}$ NASA capacity-collapse error by $63\%$ relative to EKF.

We believe this work is well suited to *Journal of Energy Storage* because it bridges electrochemical degradation physics, nonlinear observer design, and deterministic embedded deployment in battery management systems (BMS). This topic lies at the intersection of electrochemistry, control engineering, and embedded cyber-physical systems covered by the journal's scope. The framework provides chemistry-agnostic, physics-informed estimation tools and standalone C99 kernels requiring no auxiliary sensors, making it directly applicable to practitioners across electric vehicle traction packs, stationary grid storage, and multi-cell battery modules.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and agree with its content. Datasets used in this study are publicly available (XJTU Batch-5 and NASA Ames PCoE); full simulation pipelines, impedance fitting scripts, and ANSI-C99 kernels are openly available at `https://github.com/HuyHoangLe0201/SOC-Observer-PINN-Benchmark`.
