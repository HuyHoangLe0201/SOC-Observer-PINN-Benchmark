\begin{abstract}
Reliable state-of-charge (SOC) estimation is foundational to battery management systems (BMS) in electric vehicles and grid storage, governing operational safety, range prediction, and power control. In field service, however, progressive aging down to $80\%$ state-of-health (SOH), thermal extremes ($0^\circ\text{C}\text{--}45^\circ\text{C}$), flat voltage plateaus in lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells, and series-pack inconsistency severely distort internal impedance and collapse voltage observability. Because existing comparative studies predominantly evaluate fresh cells at $25^\circ\text{C}$ on desktop computers, how classical observers and machine-learning models fail across these coupled stressors---and whether they can execute on embedded microcontrollers---remains poorly understood. Here we establish a dual-tier experimental and co-simulation benchmark evaluating six representative model-based and neural estimators across ten operational stress phases, and propose a hybrid Physics-Informed Dual Kalman Filter (PIDKF) that couples online parameter adaptation with a lightweight physics-constrained neural compensator. Our comparison reveals that fixed-parameter filters diverge sharply under aging ($45.7$-fold error surge at $80\%$ SOH) while standalone neural networks suffer poor thermal generalization and high computational overhead; conversely, online parameter and hysteresis tracking stabilizes flat plateaus and multi-cell packs, and PIDKF achieves the optimal accuracy--latency trade-off with sub-$0.5\%$ aged root-mean-square error in $14.20\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$ per step on an ARM Cortex-M4 microcontroller.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The global transition toward electrified transportation and renewable grid storage relies heavily on high-energy-density lithium-ion battery (LIB) packs~\cite{Tarascon2001,Lu2013review}. Within automotive battery management systems (BMS), the State-of-Charge (SOC)---defined as the ratio of remaining usable charge to actual calibrated capacity---serves as the foundational state variable governing driving-range prediction, regenerative braking allocation, over-charge and over-discharge protection, and dynamic State-of-Power (SOP) enveloping~\cite{Plett2004part1,Xiong2018joint,Farmann2016peukert}. Unlike terminal voltage or surface temperature, however, internal lithium stoichiometry cannot be measured directly during vehicle operation because of coupled solid-state diffusion, interfacial charge-transfer kinetics, and phase transitions within porous insertion electrodes~\cite{Doyle1993,Torchio2016pybam}. Direct open-loop Coulomb counting offers an $\mathcal{O}(1)$ baseline~\cite{Plett2004part1} but accumulates unbounded drift from current-sensor offset, analog-to-digital converter (ADC) quantization, and parasitic side reactions, whereas static open-circuit voltage (OCV) lookup requires prolonged relaxation ($30\text{--}120\text{ min}$) to extinguish diffusion overpotentials~\cite{Roscher2011hysteresis,Verbrugge2004hysteresis}. Consequently, reliable real-time SOC tracking depends on closed-loop mathematical observers or learning architectures that infer internal electrochemical states from noisy terminal current, voltage, and temperature telemetry~\cite{Plett2004part2,Hu2012ecm,Ren2019aging}.

To balance physical interpretability with real-time tractability, model-based nonlinear observers predominantly pair recursive state estimation with lumped equivalent circuit models (ECMs), such as first-order resistor--capacitor (1-RC) Thevenin and second-order (2-RC) dual-polarization networks~\cite{He2011ecm,Hu2012ecm}. The industry-standard Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) linearizes the nonlinear OCV--SOC curve via first-order Taylor expansion~\cite{Plett2004part3,Santhanagopalan2006jes}, whereas the Adaptive EKF (AEKF) updates noise covariances online via Sage--Husa recursions~\cite{Xiang2022aekf,Liu2021tcn}, and higher-order sigma-point formulations---notably the Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) and Cubature Kalman Filter (CKF)---propagate deterministic sigma points to reduce linearization truncation error~\cite{Plett2006ukf,He2013ukf,Li2021ackf}. To counter parameter drift over a battery's service life, the Dual EKF (DEKF) co-estimates fast dynamic states alongside slow variations in effective series resistance $R_0$ and actual usable capacity $Q_{\text{act}}$~\cite{Charkhgard2010dekf,Yang2024degradation}, while Sliding Mode Observers (SMO) drive voltage estimation errors onto a switching manifold with guaranteed disturbance rejection~\cite{Kim2011,Chen2016smo,Huangfu2018smo}. At higher physical fidelity, continuum electrochemical formulations---spanning the pseudo-two-dimensional (P2D) Doyle--Fuller--Newman model~\cite{Doyle1993,Torchio2016pybam,Northrop2014p2d}, the Single Particle Model (SPM), and the electrolyte-extended SPM (SPMe)---support partial differential equation (PDE) observers~\cite{DiDomenico2010spm,Moura2017pde,Marquis2019spme}, yet their spatial discretization burden remains prohibitive for low-cost automotive processors.

Seeking to bypass explicit circuit modeling, data-driven machine learning approaches---including Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM), Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), and self-attention Transformer architectures---learn direct sequence-to-state mappings from historical voltage, current, and temperature windows~\cite{Hansen2005svr,Chemali2018dnn,Liu2020gpr,Song2019bilstm,Jiao2020attn,Liu2021tcn,Vaswani2017attention,Shen2025kf}. Purely black-box networks, however, lack physical conservation invariance and often extrapolate poorly under unseen thermal or aging conditions. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) address this limitation by embedding differential-algebraic physical constraints---specifically Faradaic charge conservation, Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL), and thermodynamic monotonicity---into the training objective via automatic differentiation across collocation domains~\cite{Raissi2019pinn,Brancato2026,Li2021oneshot}.

Despite rapid progress across these algorithmic families, a critical disconnect persists between idealized literature benchmarks and real-world BMS operation. Most comparative studies evaluate only fresh cells (state-of-health, $\text{SOH} = 100\%$) near room temperature ($25^\circ\text{C}$)~\cite{Hu2012ecm,Chemali2018dnn,Le2026charging}, overlooking how progressive solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) film growth and loss of active material down to $80\%$ SOH alter internal impedance and capacity. Equally important, polarization resistances scale exponentially across cold ($0^\circ\text{C}$) and elevated ($45^\circ\text{C}$) temperatures via Arrhenius kinetics~\cite{He2011ecm,Ren2019aging}; when uncompensated observers or partial parameter filters encounter large cold-weather charge-transfer overpotentials, they misattribute these voltage drops to ohmic resistance or OCV shifts, inducing severe parameter cross-coupling and state divergence.

Rankings established on steep-sloped lithium nickel manganese cobalt oxide (NMC) cells also fail to carry over to $\text{LiFePO}_4$ (LFP) chemistry, whose biphasic transition produces a wide, flat voltage plateau ($d\text{OCV}/d\text{SOC} \approx 0$) and pronounced dynamic hysteresis that collapse classical voltage observability~\cite{Roscher2011hysteresis,Verbrugge2004hysteresis,Marongiu2016hyst} unless auxiliary mechanical force sensors are introduced~\cite{Tian2025}. In vehicular service, estimators must simultaneously withstand Hall-effect current-sensor offset drift, inverter pulse-width modulation (PWM) electromagnetic interference (EMI)~\cite{Hashemi2020,Zheng2018inconsistency}, and cell-to-cell thermal and aging inconsistency across series-connected battery packs~\cite{Yang2024degradation,Zheng2018inconsistency}. Moreover, because modern deep and physics-informed networks are predominantly evaluated on desktop workstations~\cite{Song2019bilstm,Bhattacharya2024gpr}, their cycle-accurate execution latency and memory footprint on embedded microcontroller units (MCUs; ARM Cortex-M4/M7) remain largely unquantified.

To bridge these gaps, this work establishes a unified dual-tier comparative benchmark evaluating six representative paradigms---EKF, AEKF, DEKF, SMO, attention-augmented BiLSTM (BiLSTM-Attn), and PINN-based SOC estimation (PINN-SOC)---alongside a newly proposed hybrid Physics-Informed Dual Kalman Filter (PIDKF). Combining experimental cycler datasets with calibrated electro-thermal-aging co-simulation across four standardized driving schedules---the Urban Dynamometer Driving Schedule (UDDS), the US06 Supplemental Federal Test Procedure (US06), the Dynamic Stress Test (DST), and the Federal Urban Driving Schedule (FUDS)---we systematically investigate ten operational stress phases: nominal tracking, progressive aging ($100\%\rightarrow 80\%$ SOH) quantified via an Aging Sensitivity Factor (ASF), thermal extremes ($0^\circ\text{C}\text{--}45^\circ\text{C}$), large initialization uncertainty ($\Delta\text{SOC}_0 = -40\%$), multi-attribute radar trade-offs, LFP flat-plateau tracking, combined current-bias and inverter EMI corruption, model-based electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) Nyquist analysis with constant-phase element (CPE) kinetics, eight-cell series-connected (8S) pack inconsistency, and bare-metal MCU profiling using the Cortex Microcontroller Software Interface Standard digital signal processing library (CMSIS-DSP).

Through this comparative investigation, we demonstrate why fixed-parameter filters suffer a 45.7-fold root-mean-square error (RMSE) surge as charge-transfer resistance ($R_{\text{ct}}$) expands with aging, why standalone neural models struggle with out-of-distribution thermal regimes and embedded compute budgets, and how coupling online dual parameter tracking with dynamic hysteresis and a lightweight Butler--Volmer-constrained neural compensator enables PIDKF to maintain sub-$0.5\%$ aged RMSE while executing in $14.20\;\upmu\text{s}$ per step on an ARM Cortex-M4. The remainder of this paper formulates the coupled electro-thermal-aging battery model and LFP observability theory in Section~2, details the benchmark estimators and proposed hybrid PIDKF in Section~3, describes the experimental and co-simulation platform in Section~4, presents the ten-phase comparative results in Section~5, distills industrial BMS selection guidelines in Section~6, and summarizes the main conclusions in Section~7.

\section{Electrochemical Battery Modeling and Degradation Dynamics}
This section formulates the coupled electro-thermal and aging dynamics of NMC and LFP cells used throughout our benchmark. We first establish the discrete-time equivalent circuit and Arrhenius thermal balance, then define the structural parameter-allocation boundary separating the aging plant from the observers, and finally analyze OCV--SOC monotonicity, dynamic hysteresis, and flat-plateau observability under persistent excitation.

\subsection{Second-Order Dual Polarization (2-RC) Model Formulation}
The electrical response of a lithium-ion cell is modeled using a second-order Dual Polarization equivalent circuit, widely recognized as an effective compromise between physical fidelity and real-time tractability~\cite{Hu2012ecm,Xiong2014}. The circuit couples an open-circuit voltage source $V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC})$ in series with an effective ohmic series resistance $R_0 = R_{\text{ohm}} + R_{\text{sei}}$ (combining metallic ohmic resistance $R_{\text{ohm}}$ and SEI film resistance $R_{\text{sei}}$) and two parallel resistor--capacitor ($R_i \parallel C_i$, $i \in \{1, 2\}$) branches representing fast interfacial charge-transfer kinetics and slower solid-state lithium diffusion, respectively:
\begin{align}
\frac{d\,\text{SOC}(t)}{dt} &= -\frac{\eta(T)}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}}\,I(t), \label{eqn:soc_diff}\\
\frac{d U_1(t)}{dt} &= -\frac{U_1(t)}{R_1 C_1} + \frac{I(t)}{C_1}, \quad \frac{d U_2(t)}{dt} = -\frac{U_2(t)}{R_2 C_2} + \frac{I(t)}{C_2}, \label{eqn:u12_diff}
\end{align}
where $t$ is continuous time ($\text{s}$), $I(t)$ is load current (positive during discharge), $U_1(t)$ and $U_2(t)$ are the activation and concentration polarization overpotentials ($\text{V}$), $\eta(T)$ is the Faradaic coulombic efficiency at absolute cell temperature $T$ ($\text{K}$), and $Q_{\text{act}}$ is the usable capacity ($\text{A}\cdot\text{h}$). By Kirchhoff's voltage law, terminal voltage $V_t(t)$ obeys:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:vt_diff}
V_t(t) = V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC}(t)) - U_1(t) - U_2(t) - I(t) R_0,
\end{equation}
so resistive and polarization voltage drops strictly depress terminal voltage under discharge while guaranteeing nonnegative irreversible entropy production in accordance with the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

Discretizing Eqs.~\eqref{eqn:soc_diff}--\eqref{eqn:u12_diff} at sampling interval $\Delta t = 1.0\text{ s}$ and step index $k$ via zero-order hold (ZOH) on the piecewise-constant current $I_k$ yields the discrete-time state-space representation with polarization time constants $\tau_i = R_i C_i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:state_space_discrete}
\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{A}_k \mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{B}_k I_k + \mathbf{w}_k,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_k = [\text{SOC}_k, U_{1,k}, U_{2,k}]^T$ is the discrete state vector (with superscript $T$ denoting matrix transpose), $\mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}_k)$ is zero-mean Gaussian process noise with covariance matrix $\mathbf{Q}_k$, and the diagonal transition matrix ($\operatorname{diag}$) and input vector are:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{A}_k &= \operatorname{diag}\left(1, e^{-\Delta t/\tau_1}, e^{-\Delta t/\tau_2}\right), \label{eqn:Ak_mat} \\
\mathbf{B}_k &= \begin{bmatrix}
-\frac{\eta \Delta t}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}}, & R_1\left(1 - e^{-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_1}}\right), & R_2\left(1 - e^{-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_2}}\right)
\end{bmatrix}^T, \label{eqn:Bk_mat}
\end{align}
with nominal parameters calibrated in Table~\ref{tab:ecm_params} (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}). The corresponding nonlinear voltage measurement model is:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:measurement_eq}
y_k = V_k = V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC}_k) - U_{1,k} - U_{2,k} - I_k R_0 + v_k,
\end{equation}
where $y_k = V_k$ is the sampled terminal voltage and $v_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_v)$ denotes zero-mean Gaussian measurement noise with scalar variance $R_v$.

\subsection{Dynamic Electro-Thermal Balance and Arrhenius Kinetics}
To capture temperature-dependent kinetics, cell core temperature $T(t)$ evolves according to a lumped thermal energy balance:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:thermal_balance}
C_{\text{th}} \frac{d T(t)}{dt} = \dot{Q}_{\text{gen}}(t) - \frac{T(t) - T_{\text{amb}}}{R_{\text{th}}},
\end{equation}
where $C_{\text{th}}$ is the lumped thermal capacitance, $R_{\text{th}}$ is the equivalent thermal resistance to ambient temperature $T_{\text{amb}}$, and total heat generation $\dot{Q}_{\text{gen}}(t)$ combines irreversible Joule and polarization dissipation with reversible entropic heat governed by the entropic potential coefficient $\partial V_{\text{ocv}}/\partial T$. Because potentiometric titration shows a predominantly negative entropic slope across normalized state-of-charge $z \equiv \text{SOC}$, reversible heating remains exothermic over most of discharge and reverses sign only near full charge in NMC cells. For both cylindrical cell formats examined here, a small Biot number ($\text{Bi} \ll 1$) keeps core-to-surface gradients minimal even under sustained $2\text{C}$ discharge (where $n\text{C}$ scales current relative to initial capacity $Q_{\text{init}}$), justifying a single-lump core temperature linked to surface temperature $T_s$ through conductive resistance $R_{\text{cond}}$.

Internal resistances vary strongly with temperature via piecewise Arrhenius kinetics characterized by cold- and warm-regime activation energies $E_{a,i}^{\text{cold}}$ and $E_{a,i}^{\text{hot}}$ (reflecting lithium-ion desolvation barriers at sub-ambient temperatures), referenced to baseline resistances $R_{0,\text{ref}}$ and $R_{i,\text{ref}}$ at reference temperature $T_{\text{ref}}$ using the universal gas constant $R$:
\begin{align}
R_0(T) &= R_{0,\text{ref}} f_{T,0}(T), \label{eqn:arrh_r0}\\
R_i(T) &= R_{i,\text{ref}} f_{T,i}(T), \quad i \in \{1, 2\}, \label{eqn:arrh_ri}
\end{align}
where $f_{T,i}(T)$ denotes the Arrhenius exponential scaling factor parameterized in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}. Validated against surface thermocouples across dynamic UDDS and US06 cycles, this coupled thermal model achieves sub-$0.4^\circ\text{C}$ surface temperature root-mean-square error ($\text{RMSE}_T$) for both chemistries.

\subsection{Semi-Empirical Aging Parameterization Across Degradation Regimes}
Although cell aging originates from complex microscopic mechanisms including SEI growth and active particle cracking~\cite{Birkl2017diagnostics,Ren2019aging}, real-time estimation relies on a separable macroscopic parameterization anchored to state-of-health $\text{SOH} \equiv Q_{\text{act}}(T_{\text{ref}})/Q_{\text{init}}$ relative to nominal fresh-cell resistances $R_{i,\text{nom}}$ and capacitances $C_{i,\text{nom}}$:
\begin{align}
R_0(T, \text{SOH}) &= R_{0,\text{nom}} f_{T,0}(T) [1.0 + \kappa_{R_0}\,(1.0 - \text{SOH})], \label{eqn:aging_r0}\\
R_1(T, \text{SOH}) &= R_{1,\text{nom}} f_{T,1}(T) [1.0 + \kappa_{R_1}\,(1.0 - \text{SOH})], \label{eqn:aging_r1}\\
R_2(T, \text{SOH}) &= R_{2,\text{nom}} f_{T,2}(T) [1.0 + \kappa_{R_2}\,(1.0 - \text{SOH})], \label{eqn:aging_r2}\\
Q_{\text{act}}(T, \text{SOH}) &= \text{SOH} \cdot Q_{\text{init}} f_Q(T), \label{eqn:aging_q}
\end{align}
where $\kappa_{R_i}$ are resistance degradation sensitivity factors, $f_Q(T)$ captures sub-ambient kinetic capacity attenuation via activation energy $E_{a,Q}$ and warm stoichiometric recovery via coefficient $\kappa_{T,\text{hot}}$, and double-layer capacitances $C_i$ decay linearly with $(1-\text{SOH})$ at rates $\gamma_{C,i}$. Under realistic field-BMS current offset bias $I_{\text{bias}}$, branch resistance mismatch $\delta R_i$, and root-mean-square (RMS) load current $I_{\text{RMS}}$, worst-case unmodeled branch overpotential disturbance $\tilde{D}_{U_i}$ remains comfortably inside the sliding disturbance bound $\Phi_d$ across all thermal and aging regimes (calibrated constants and metrology tolerances are detailed in Section~\ref{sec:exp_setup} and Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}).

Crucially, we enforce a strict structural parameter allocation between the physical battery plant and the compared estimators. Whereas aging modulates every circuit parameter in the plant, fixed-parameter observers (EKF, AEKF, SMO) lock parameters at nominal fresh values to expose degradation drift. Conversely, DEKF tracks only the orthogonal parameter vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k = [\hat{R}_{0,k}, 1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}]^T$ while freezing the RC branches to avoid parameter collinearity; the resulting unmodeled polarization mismatch $\delta V_{\text{pol},k}^*$ between plant states $U_{i,k}^{\text{plant}}$ and fixed-RC model states $\hat{U}_{i,k}^{\text{ECM}}$ is precisely what the proposed PIDKF compensates online.

\subsection{OCV--SOC Monotonicity and Dynamic Hysteresis}
To preclude spurious local extrema in gradient-based observers, the NMC open-circuit potential $V_{\text{ocv}}(z)$ is calibrated as a strictly monotonic fifth-order polynomial:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:ocv_poly}
V_{\text{ocv}}(z) = p_5 z^5 + p_4 z^4 + p_3 z^3 + p_2 z^2 + p_1 z + p_0,
\end{equation}
whose coefficients $p_0,\dots,p_5$ (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}) yield complex-conjugate derivative roots (with imaginary unit $j = \sqrt{-1}$), guaranteeing a strictly positive slope bounded below by $\alpha_{\text{NMC}} > 0$ up to maximum slope $V'_{\max}$ and ensuring global invertibility via the Inverse Function Theorem. For this three-state system, the discrete observability matrix $\mathcal{O}_k$ admits the Vandermonde determinant:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:det_obs}
\det(\mathcal{O}_k) = V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k) (1 - a_1)(1 - a_2)(a_1 - a_2),
\end{equation}
where $a_i \equiv e^{-\Delta t/\tau_i}$ denote distinct RC decay poles. Because $\tau_1 \ne \tau_2$ and $V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k) \ge \alpha_{\text{NMC}}$ across NMC operation, $\mathcal{O}_k$ maintains full rank everywhere. In stark contrast, when $V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k)$ collapses toward zero along the LFP plateau, the condition number $\kappa(\mathcal{O}_k) \equiv \|\mathcal{O}_k\|_2 \|\mathcal{O}_k^{-1}\|_2$ surges by nearly an order of magnitude, revealing the analytical root cause of flat-plateau estimation sensitivity.

Physically, LFP undergoes a two-phase transition ($\text{LiFePO}_4 \leftrightarrow \text{FePO}_4$) that, under the Gibbs phase rule at fixed temperature and pressure, pins lithium chemical potential across intermediate stoichiometries and flattens the equilibrium curve near a minimal slope bound $\alpha_{\text{LFP}} > 0$ while generating pronounced path-dependent hysteresis~\cite{Roscher2011hysteresis,Verbrugge2004hysteresis}. We represent this dynamic hysteresis overpotential $V_h(t)$ using a single-state decay model parameterized by dimensionless rate $\beta$ and plateau envelope $M(z) \equiv M_0$ (bounded by upper limit $M_{\max}$):
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:hysteresis_diff}
\frac{d V_h(t)}{dt} = -\frac{\beta \eta |I(t)|}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}(T, \text{SOH})} \left[ V_h(t) - M(z) \operatorname{sgn}(I(t)) \right],
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sgn}(\cdot)$ is the signum function. Exact ZOH integration over one sampling step with SOC increment $|\Delta z_k|$ yields a contractive convex update governed by hysteresis decay factor $a_{h,k} \equiv e^{-\beta |\Delta z_k|}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:hysteresis_discrete}
V_{h,k+1} = a_{h,k} V_{h,k} + \left(1 - a_{h,k}\right) M(z_k) \operatorname{sgn}(I_k),
\end{equation}
which guarantees that $V_{h,k}$ remains positively invariant within $[-M_{\max}, M_{\max}]$. Augmenting the LFP state vector to $\mathbf{x}_k^{\text{LFP}} = [\text{SOC}_k,\, U_{1,k},\, U_{2,k},\, V_{h,k}]^T$ gives the four-state discrete system:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:x_lfp}
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{\text{LFP}} = \mathbf{A}_k^{\text{LFP}} \mathbf{x}_k^{\text{LFP}} + \mathbf{B}_k^{\text{LFP}} I_k + \mathbf{g}_k + \mathbf{w}_k,
\end{equation}
where $[B_{1,k}, B_{2,k}, B_{3,k}]^T = \mathbf{B}_k$ from Eq.~\eqref{eqn:Bk_mat}, and the augmented operators are:
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{A}_k^{\text{LFP}} &= \operatorname{diag}(1, a_1, a_2, a_{h,k}), \qquad
\mathbf{B}_k^{\text{LFP}} = [B_{1,k},\, B_{2,k},\, B_{3,k},\, 0]^T, \\
\mathbf{g}_k &= \left[0,\, 0,\, 0,\, (1 - a_{h,k}) M(z_k) \operatorname{sgn}(I_k)\right]^T.
\end{align*}
Terminal voltage becomes:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:vt_lfp}
V_{t,k} = V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC}_k) - V_{h,k} - U_{1,k} - U_{2,k} - I_k R_0 + v_k,
\end{equation}
and over short frozen-coefficient windows ($a_h \equiv a_{h,k}$), the augmented local observability determinant expands to:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:det_obs_lfp}
|\det(\mathcal{O}_{4,k})| = V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k)(1 - a_h)|a_1 - a_2|\prod_{i=1}^2 (1 - a_i)|a_i - a_h|.
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eqn:det_obs_lfp} exposes an essential dichotomy between rest and dynamic excitation. When the cell rests ($I_k = 0$), the hysteresis pole collapses to unity ($a_{h,k} = 1$), becoming collinear with the SOC integrator mode and rendering static hysteresis unobservable from voltage alone; consequently, parameter adaptation is frozen ($\mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} = \mathbf{0}$ for parameter gain $\mathbf{K}_{\theta,k}$) during open-circuit rest. Conversely, under Persistent Excitation (PE, non-vanishing window-averaged current), $a_{h,k} < 1$ separates from both unity and the RC poles except at isolated singleton resonance currents $\mathcal{I}_{\text{res}}$ of Lebesgue measure zero ($\mu_L(\mathcal{I}_{\text{res}}) = 0$). Evaluated over dynamic $30\text{ s}$ windows, the normalized-state ($\tilde{\mathbf{x}}$) linear time-varying (LTV) observability Gramian $\mathcal{G}_o \succeq \alpha_o \mathbf{I}$ (with uniform floor $\alpha_o > 0$ and identity matrix $\mathbf{I}$) achieves a strictly positive minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{G}_o)$ across all four driving schedules (Appendix~\ref{app:jacobians}). Figure~\ref{fig:0_mechanism} summarizes the coupled microscopic degradation physics, the augmented equivalent circuit architecture, and the resulting electrochemical observability landscape across both chemistries.

\section{State-of-Charge Estimation Methodologies}
This section formulates the six representative benchmark estimators---EKF, AEKF, DEKF, SMO, BiLSTM-Attn, and PINN-SOC---within a unified discrete-time framework before introducing the proposed hybrid PIDKF. For PIDKF, we establish causal target residual generation, exogenous Jacobian sensitivity decoupling, Butler--Volmer passivity constraints, and closed-loop ultimate error boundedness under intermittent driving excitation.

\subsection{Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)}
Within the nonlinear Kalman filtering family, higher-order sigma-point variants such as UKF and CKF~\cite{Plett2006ukf,He2013ukf} propagate deterministic sigma points via scaled unscented transform (UT) coefficients ($\alpha_{\text{UT}}$, $\beta_{\text{UT}}$, $\kappa_{\text{UT}}$) over state dimension $n$ to avoid explicit Jacobian evaluation. Evaluated under identical process and measurement noise covariances ($\mathbf{Q}, R_v$), UKF mirrors EKF accuracy on fresh cells while incurring more than three times the embedded execution latency (Table~\ref{tab:8_embedded}), and still diverges at comparable magnitude under severe aging because parameter drift overwhelms quadrature refinements. Consequently, EKF serves as the representative non-adaptive baseline.

At each step, EKF linearizes the terminal voltage observation model around the \emph{a priori} state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-$ via the observation Jacobian $\mathbf{H}_k$ (where superscripts $-$ and $+$ denote \emph{a priori} and \emph{a posteriori} quantities, respectively)~\cite{Plett2004part3}:
\begin{align}\label{eqn:ekf_jacobian}
\mathbf{H}_k^{\text{NMC}} &= \begin{bmatrix} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^-) & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \nonumber\\
\mathbf{H}_k^{\text{LFP}} &= \begin{bmatrix} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^-) & -1 & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix},
\end{align}
where the trailing negative entry captures subtractive hysteresis overpotential in LFP cells. The recursive cycle alternates between a time-prediction stage:
\begin{align}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^- &= \mathbf{A}_{k-1} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}^+ + \mathbf{B}_{k-1} I_{k-1} + \mathbf{g}_{k-1}, \label{eqn:ekf_pred_state}\\
\mathbf{P}_k^- &= \mathbf{A}_{k-1} \mathbf{P}_{k-1}^+ \mathbf{A}_{k-1}^T + \mathbf{Q}, \label{eqn:ekf_pred_cov}
\end{align}
and a measurement-correction stage updating the state and covariance $\mathbf{P}_k^+$:
\begin{align}
e_k &= V_k - \hat{V}_{t,k}^-, \quad \mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T \left( \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T + R_v \right)^{-1}, \label{eqn:ekf_gain}\\
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^+ &= \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^- + \mathbf{K}_k e_k, \label{eqn:ekf_upd_state}\\
\mathbf{P}_k^+ &= (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k) \mathbf{P}_k^- (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k)^T + \mathbf{K}_k R_v \mathbf{K}_k^T, \label{eqn:ekf_joseph}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{P}_k^-$ and $\mathbf{P}_k^+$ denote the \emph{a priori} and \emph{a posteriori} state error covariances, $\mathbf{g}_{k-1}$ vanishes for NMC and follows Eq.~\eqref{eqn:hysteresis_discrete} for LFP, $e_k$ is the voltage innovation relative to predicted terminal voltage $\hat{V}_{t,k}^-$, and $\mathbf{K}_k$ is the Kalman gain with identity matrix $\mathbf{I}$. Equation~\eqref{eqn:ekf_joseph} implements the stabilized Joseph covariance update to guarantee matrix symmetry and positive-definiteness ($\mathbf{P}_k^+ \succ 0$) under finite-precision arithmetic.

\subsection{Adaptive Extended Kalman Filter (AEKF)}
Fixed noise covariances degrade accuracy whenever sensor noise or unmodeled polarization shifts across regimes. The AEKF augments EKF with a Sage--Husa (SH) online adaptive noise estimator~\cite{Xiang2022aekf}:
\begin{align}
\hat{r}_k &= (1 - d_k)\hat{r}_{k-1} + d_k \left( V_k - \hat{V}_{t,k}^- \right), \label{eqn:sh_mean}\\
\hat{R}_{v,k} &= (1 - d_k)\hat{R}_{v,k-1} + d_k \left( e_k^2 - \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T \right), \label{eqn:sh_meas_cov}
\end{align}
where $\hat{r}_k$ is the estimated innovation bias, $\hat{R}_{v,k}$ is the adaptive scalar measurement noise variance, and $d_k$ is an exponential weighting sequence controlled by forgetting factor $\lambda_{\text{SH}}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}). Because process covariance $\mathbf{Q}$ and measurement variance $R_v$ cannot both be uniquely identified from a single scalar innovation sequence, $\mathbf{Q}$ remains fixed at its calibrated baseline while $\hat{R}_{v,k}$ is projected onto a strictly positive lower variance floor $R_{v,\min} > 0$ to preserve positive-definiteness.

\subsection{Dual Extended Kalman Filter (DEKF)}
To separate fast state dynamics from slow parameter evolution, DEKF couples two interacting Kalman filters in parallel~\cite{Plett2004part3,Charkhgard2010dekf}. A fast state filter updates $\mathbf{x}_k$ conditioned on the current parameter estimate $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k = [\hat{R}_{0,k}, 1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}]^T$, while a concurrent parameter filter models degradation as a slow random walk $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{\theta}_k + \mathbf{w}_{\theta,k}$ driven by parameter process noise $\mathbf{w}_{\theta,k} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}_\theta)$ with covariance $\mathbf{Q}_\theta$. Accounting for lagged state sensitivity through total Fr\'echet differentiation yields the parameter sensitivity vector $\mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:dekf_param_jacobian}
\mathbf{C}_{\theta,k} = \begin{bmatrix} -I_k & -\frac{\eta \Delta t I_{k-1}}{3600} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^+) \end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
whose second entry captures how inverse-capacity updates propagate through the \emph{a posteriori} OCV slope. Driven by scalar parameter innovation variance $R_{\theta,v}$ and parameter covariances $\mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^-$ and $\mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^+$, the parameter filter updates recursively via the stabilized Joseph form:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} &= \mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^- \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}^T [ \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k} \mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^- \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}^T + R_{\theta,v} ]^{-1}, \label{eqn:dekf_param_gain}\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k^+ &= \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k^- + \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} e_k, \label{eqn:dekf_param_upd}\\
\mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^+ &= (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}) \mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^- (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k})^T + \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} R_{\theta,v} \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k}^T. \label{eqn:dekf_param_cov}
\end{align}

\subsection{Sliding Mode Observer (SMO)}
The SMO enforces robustness against bounded model-plant mismatch by driving voltage tracking errors onto a boundary layer of thickness $\mu$ around the sliding manifold $S_k = V_{\text{meas},k} - \hat{V}_{t,k} = 0$, where $V_{\text{meas},k}$ is the measured terminal voltage~\cite{Kim2011,Chen2016smo}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:smo_state_eq}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1} = \mathbf{A}_k \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k + \mathbf{B}_k I_k + \mathbf{g}_k + \mathbf{L}(\hat{z}_k) \operatorname{sat}\left( \frac{S_k}{\mu} \right),
\end{equation}
with continuous saturation function $\operatorname{sat}(\cdot)$ and scheduled state-feedback injection vector $\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}_k)$. To prevent gain explosion across flat plateaus while preserving reachability on steep OCV slopes, switching injection acts exclusively along the SOC coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}) = \left[\frac{l_{\text{smo}}}{\max(\alpha_{\min}, V_{\text{ocv}}^\prime(\hat{z}))},\, 0,\, 0,\, (0)\right]^T,
\end{equation}
where $l_{\text{smo}}$ is the scalar injection gain, $\alpha_{\min}$ is the minimum derivative regularization threshold, and the parenthesized fourth entry applies to the four-state LFP model (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}). Whenever $V_{\text{ocv}}^\prime(\hat{z}) \ge \alpha_{\min}$, the effective manifold gain $\gamma(\hat{z}) \equiv \mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}) = l_{\text{smo}}$ strictly exceeds the nominal disturbance bound $\Phi_d$, while setting polarization and hysteresis injection gains to zero ($l_2 = l_3 = l_h = 0$) lets internal overpotentials evolve along passive dissipative dynamics without chattering (discrete quasi-sliding mode proof in Appendix~\ref{app:lyapunov}).

\subsection{Bidirectional LSTM with Self-Attention (BiLSTM-Attn)}
As the data-driven sequence baseline, BiLSTM-Attn maps a causal sliding window of current, voltage, and temperature measurements:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{X}_k = [I_{k-W:k},\, V_{k-W:k},\, T_{k-W:k}] \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times 3},
\end{equation}
of look-back length $W$ (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}) into forward ($\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t$) and backward ($\overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t$) hidden representations of dimension $D$ per direction~\cite{Song2019bilstm,Vaswani2017attention}. Concatenating both directions (denoted $\|$) into $\mathbf{h}_t = [\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t \,\|\, \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{2 D}$, a temporal self-attention layer computes alignment scores $u_t$ and normalized attention weights $\alpha_t$ via learnable weight matrix $\mathbf{W}_a$, bias $\mathbf{b}_a$, and projection vector $\mathbf{v}$:
\begin{align}
u_t &= \mathbf{v}^T \tanh(\mathbf{W}_a \mathbf{h}_t + \mathbf{b}_a), \quad \alpha_t = \frac{\exp(u_t)}{\sum_{j=1}^W \exp(u_j)}, \label{eqn:attn_weight}\\
\mathbf{c}_k &= \sum_{t=1}^W \alpha_t \mathbf{h}_t, \label{eqn:attn_context}
\end{align}
yielding a compact context vector $\mathbf{c}_k$ decoded through fully connected layers into $\hat{z}_k$.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN-SOC)}
Rather than treating SOC estimation as unconstrained regression, PINN-SOC embeds physical conservation laws directly into the neural training graph~\cite{Raissi2019pinn,Brancato2026,Li2021oneshot}. The network $\mathcal{NN}_1$ ingests measurable telemetry $\mathbf{u}_k = [t_k, I_k, V_{t,k}, T_k, \hat{\text{SOH}}_{\text{latched}}]^T$ augmented with a quasi-stationary capacity health flag $\hat{\text{SOH}}_{\text{latched}}$ periodically updated during constant-current charging~\cite{Le2026charging}. Because capacity fade evolves orders of magnitude more slowly than individual driving cycles, battery charge depletion satisfies the governing differential conservation law:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:pinn_ode}
\frac{d\,\text{SOC}(t)}{dt} = -\frac{\eta(T, I)\, I(t)}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}(T, \text{SOH})}.
\end{equation}
Evaluating the predicted trajectory via automatic differentiation yields the chain-rule time derivative $d\hat{z}/dt$ and Faradaic conservation residual $\mathcal{R}_{\text{Faraday}}(t)$:
\begin{align}
\frac{d\hat{z}(t)}{dt} &= \frac{\partial \mathcal{NN}_1}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial \mathcal{NN}_1}{\partial I} \frac{dI}{dt} + \frac{\partial \mathcal{NN}_1}{\partial V_t} \frac{dV_t}{dt} + \frac{\partial \mathcal{NN}_1}{\partial T} \frac{dT}{dt}, \label{eqn:pinn_chain}\\
\mathcal{R}_{\text{Faraday}}(t) &= \frac{d\hat{z}(t)}{dt} + \frac{\eta(T, I)\, I(t)}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}(T, \text{SOH})}, \label{eqn:res_faraday}
\end{align}
where telemetry time derivatives are evaluated offline via smoothing splines during training while online inference remains strictly causal.

Simultaneously, because internal polarization and hysteresis voltages ($\hat{V}_{\text{pol}} \equiv \hat{U}_1 + \hat{U}_2 + \hat{V}_h$) cannot be measured directly in field packs, PINN-SOC enforces terminal-voltage Kirchhoff consistency across the residual manifold:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:res_kvl}
\mathcal{R}_{\text{KVL}}(t) = V_{\text{meas}}(t) - \left[ V_{\text{ocv}}(\hat{z}(t)) - I(t) R_0(T, \text{SOH}) - \hat{V}_{\text{pol}}(t) \right],
\end{equation}
while an output Sigmoid activation guarantees bounded estimates $\hat{z} \in (0, 1)$. During offline optimization over parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{PINN}}$, supervised data loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}$ across $N_d$ labeled samples (with reference state $\text{SOC}_k^{\text{ref}}$ anchored by periodic Reference Performance Tests, RPT) is balanced against physics residuals ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Faraday}}, \mathcal{L}_{\text{KVL}}, \mathcal{L}_{\text{mono}}$) weighted by coefficients $\lambda_{\text{Faraday}}, \lambda_{\text{KVL}}, \lambda_{\text{mono}}$ over $N_c$ unlabeled collocation points:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:pinn_loss}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{PINN}}) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} + \lambda_{\text{Faraday}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Faraday}} + \lambda_{\text{KVL}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{KVL}} + \lambda_{\text{mono}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{mono}},
\end{equation}
where individual components enforce trajectory, differential, algebraic, and quasi-stationary monotonicity ($\partial \hat{z}/\partial V_t \ge 0$):
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} &= \frac{1}{N_d} \sum_{k=1}^{N_d} (\hat{z}_k - \text{SOC}_k^{\text{ref}})^2, \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{Faraday}} = \frac{1}{N_c} \sum_{j=1}^{N_c} \|\mathcal{R}_{\text{Faraday}}(t_j)\|^2, \label{eqn:pinn_loss_data_faraday} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{KVL}} &= \frac{1}{N_c} \sum_{j=1}^{N_c} \|\mathcal{R}_{\text{KVL}}(t_j)\|^2, \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{mono}} = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{j=1}^{N_c} \left[\max\left(0, -\frac{\partial \hat{z}_j}{\partial V_{t,j}}\right)\right]^2. \label{eqn:pinn_loss_kvl_mono}
\end{align}
Trained as a four-layer multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU) activations via decoupled weight-decay Adam (AdamW; hyperparameters in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}), physics regularization reduces error substantially relative to an unconstrained MLP without physical penalties (Table~\ref{tab:1_udds}).

\subsection{Proposed Hybrid Architecture: PIDKF}\label{sec:pidkf}
To unite the recursive convergence guarantees and parameter adaptability of DEKF with the nonlinear representational capacity of physics-constrained neural networks, we introduce the hybrid \textbf{PIDKF} (Fig.~\ref{fig:0_framework}). In this architecture, a DEKF backbone tracks both state $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k$ (including dynamic hysteresis $\hat{V}_{h,k}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eqn:vt_lfp} for LFP cells) and parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k = [\hat{R}_{0,k}, 1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}]^T$, while a compact feedforward compensator $\mathcal{NN}(\mathbf{u}_k; \boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}})$ with weights $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}}$ predicts the unmodeled kinetic and diffusion polarization residual $\delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}$ from online measurements and \emph{a priori} states $\mathbf{u}_k = [I_k, T_k, \hat{z}_k^-, \hat{U}_{1,k}^-, \hat{U}_{2,k}^-, \text{SOH}_{\text{ref},k}]^T$ (where $\text{SOH}_{\text{ref},k}$ removes thermal capacity factor $f_Q(T_k)$ from $\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}$). To prevent unphysical overpotential predictions, the neural output passes through a symmetric saturation operator $\operatorname{clip}(\cdot)$ bounded by a Butler--Volmer current envelope $V_{\text{pol}}^{\max}(I_k) = \kappa_0 + \kappa_1 |I_k|$ (up to rated limit $V_{\text{pol},\max}$ at maximum current $I_{\max}$, with coefficients $\kappa_0, \kappa_1 > 0$).

Strict causality separates offline calibration from online embedded inference. During offline training on Cell Batch~A, target voltage residuals $\delta V_{t,\text{target},k}$ are constructed using forward-simulated fixed-RC circuit states initialized from rest, preventing any dependence on closed-loop ground truth. During real-time BMS operation, adding $\delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}$ to the ECM terminal voltage prediction forms the PIDKF voltage innovation $e_{k,\text{PIDKF}}$ driving both state and parameter updates, while additive effective innovation variances $R_{\text{eff},k}$ and $R_{\theta,\text{eff}}$ account for orthogonal neural approximation uncertainty ($\sigma_{\text{NN}}$) and first-order linearization error ($\sigma_{\text{approx}}$) under zero-mean expectation $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ (detailed variance budget in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}).

Exact differentiation through the composite observation model adds neural sensitivity terms to both the state and parameter Jacobians:
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{H}_{\text{PIDKF},k}^{\text{exact}} = \mathbf{H}_k + \frac{\partial \delta \hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}}{\partial \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-}, \qquad
\mathbf{C}_{\theta,\text{PIDKF},k}^{\text{exact}} = \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k} + \left[0,\; \frac{\partial \mathcal{NN}}{\partial \text{SOH}_{\text{ref},k}}\left(-\frac{\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}^2}{Q_{\text{init}} f_Q(T_k)}\right)\right].
\end{equation*}
Because polarization time constants ($\tau_{\text{state}}$) are orders of magnitude faster than capacity degradation ($\tau_{\text{SOH}}$), PIDKF adopts an \emph{exogenous sensitivity decoupling} ($\mathbf{H}_{\text{PIDKF},k} \approx \mathbf{H}_k$, $\mathbf{C}_{\theta,\text{PIDKF},k} \approx \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}$) that treats SOH as quasi-static across instantaneous steps. Omitting reverse-mode backpropagation through the neural layer induces negligible Jacobian discrepancy while reducing embedded Cortex-M4 execution latency by $37.3\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:9_pidkf}).

Rather than enforcing continuous ordinary differential equation (ODE) collocation during deployment, the neural compensator acts as a Butler--Volmer-constrained overpotential surrogate trained over $N$ samples via composite objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PIDKF}}$ combining residual mean-square error $\mathcal{L}_{\text{res}}$, physics penalty $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}$, and weight decay (weighted by multipliers $\lambda_{\text{phys}}, \lambda_{\text{reg}}, \gamma_I > 0$):
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PIDKF}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}}) &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{res}} + \lambda_{\text{phys}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}} \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}}\|_2^2, \label{eqn:loss_pidkf}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N \left[\max\left(0, |\delta \hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}| - V_{\text{pol}}^{\max}\right)^2 + \gamma_I \max\left(0, I_k \delta \hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}\right)^2\right], \label{eqn:loss_pidkf_phys}
\end{align}
where residual loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{res}}$ fits predicted overpotential to $\delta V_{t,\text{target},k}$ and physics penalty $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}$ enforces thermodynamic passivity ($I_k \delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k} \le 0$) alongside the Butler--Volmer envelope. The resulting two-hidden-layer MLP occupies only $1.03$ kilobytes (KB) of Flash memory and requires $560$ floating-point operations (FLOPs) per step (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}).

Closed-loop stability of the hybrid PIDKF follows from two structural properties formalized in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}. First, hard overpotential clipping guarantees that unmodeled polarization mismatch $\varepsilon_k$ remains uniformly bounded by an analytical supremum $\bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{theo}}$ (and within a tight empirical envelope $\bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{emp}}$ across nominal service, degrading gracefully under extreme faults). Second, active drive windows of length $N$ satisfying Uniform Complete Observability (UCO) and Uniform Complete Controllability (UCC) alternate with bounded rest intervals ($T_{\text{rest},m} \le T_{\max}$), during which rest-period innovation gating ($|I_k| < I_{\text{rest,th}}$) freezes gains ($\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} = \mathbf{0}$) so that the closed-loop error transition matrix $\mathbf{F}_k \equiv \mathbf{A}_k(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k\mathbf{H}_k)$ remains non-expansive under uniformly bounded process and innovation noise ($\underline{q}, \bar{q}, \underline{r}, \bar{r}$). Consequently, multi-cycle transition products $\boldsymbol{\Phi}_F$ contract geometrically with effective decay rate $\rho < 1$, prefactor $c \ge 1$, and uniform Riccati covariance bounds ($\underline{p}\mathbf{I} \preceq \mathbf{P}_k \preceq \bar{p}\mathbf{I}$). With bounded gain, plant transition, and residual suprema ($\bar{K}, \bar{A}, \bar{\varepsilon}$), the \emph{a priori} estimation error $\mathbf{e}_k^-$ (for state dimension $n \in \{3, 4\}$ and matrix trace $\operatorname{tr}(\cdot)$) achieves discrete Input-to-State Stability (ISS) as well as first-moment and mean-square Uniformly Ultimately Bounded (UUB) convergence:
\begin{align}
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{e}_k^-]\| &\le \frac{c \bar{A} \bar{K} \bar{\varepsilon}}{1 - \rho} < \infty, \label{eqn:bibs_pidkf}\\
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{e}_k^-\|^2] &\le n\bar{p} + \left(\frac{c \bar{A} \bar{K} \bar{\varepsilon}}{1 - \rho}\right)^2 < \infty. \label{eqn:ms_pidkf}
\end{align}
Thus, even when observability vanishes temporarily at rest, any initialization offset decays exponentially upon resumed driving without steady-state bias.

\section{Experimental Benchmarking Platform and Methodology\label{sec:exp_setup}}
This section outlines the empirical cycling datasets and calibrated co-simulation environment used to evaluate generalization across unseen cells and chemistries. We detail the three-tier validation framework and bare-metal ARM Cortex-M timing setup, introduce the four standardized automotive drive schedules, and define the quantitative error, convergence, and aging-sensitivity metrics.

\subsection{Benchmark Platform and Empirical Calibration Data}
Our benchmark integrates open-access empirical cycling datasets from the Center for Advanced Life Cycle Engineering (CALCE, University of Maryland)~\cite{Pecht2011calce} and Oxford University~\cite{Birkl2017diagnostics} with a calibrated electro-thermal-aging co-simulation testbed. Two representative chemistries are evaluated with strict cell-level batch separation (Table~\ref{tab:ecm_params}): Samsung INR18650-20R (INR20R) NMC cells (\texttt{INR20R-1,2} in Batch~A; \texttt{INR20R-3,4} in Batch~B) and A123 ANR26650M1-B (ANR26650) LFP cells (\texttt{A123-1,2} in Batch~A; \texttt{A123-3,4} in Batch~B, anchored to initial usable capacity $Q_{\text{init}}$ under $1\text{C}$ discharge at $25^\circ\text{C}$)~\cite{He2011ecm,Pecht2011calce}. Standardized United States Advanced Battery Consortium (USABC) and U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) driving schedules (DST, FUDS, US06, and UDDS) are applied alongside dimensionless EIS charge-transfer degradation ratios scaled from Oxford aging trajectories~\cite{Birkl2017diagnostics}.

The evaluation spans three complementary validation tiers. First, the \emph{Experimental Baseline Tier} evaluates Phases~1, 3, and~6 directly on Arbin BT-2000 cycler measurements; research-grade auto-zero direct-current (DC) tare calibration across full-scale (F.S.) range, thermal-electromotive-force (EMF) compensation, and 2-h rest OCV anchoring via a 24-bit ADC and 4-wire Kelvin Fluke 8508A reference multimeter restrict combined reference SOC uncertainty to $\sigma_{\text{SOC,ref}} \le \pm 0.036\%$ across both chemistries. Second, the \emph{Calibrated Co-Simulation Tier} evaluates Phases~2, 4--5, and~7--10 across controlled aging down to $80\%$ SOH, thermal extremes ($0^\circ\text{C}\text{--}45^\circ\text{C}$), sensor faults, EIS spectra, 8S pack inconsistency, and MCU execution, matching empirical terminal voltages within $13.6\text{ mV}$ RMS ($<0.35\%$). Third, \emph{Supplemental Experimental Validation} (Section~\ref{sec:hw_profiling}, Table~\ref{tab:9_pidkf}) tests held-out real aged cells from CALCE ($85\%$ SOH NMC and LFP) and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Prognostics Center of Excellence (PCoE)~\cite{Saha2007nasa} under cold ($4^\circ\text{C}$, \texttt{B0041}) and hot ($43^\circ\text{C}$, \texttt{B0029}) constant-current (CC) $4\text{ A}$ discharge.

To guarantee cell- and schedule-level generalization, \emph{Cell Batch~A} provides low-rate titration and \textbf{DST} profiles across RPT checkpoints ($100\%, 90\%, 80\%$ SOH) for calibrating ECM priors and training neural models ($25^\circ\text{C}$ for baseline networks; $0^\circ\text{C}, 25^\circ\text{C}, 45^\circ\text{C}$ for PIDKF). All comparative tests evaluate strictly unseen \emph{Cell Batch~B} cells under held-out \textbf{UDDS, FUDS, and US06} schedules, reporting both inter-cell standard deviation ($\text{SD}_{\text{cell}}$) and random-seed standard deviation ($\text{SD}_{\text{seed}}$) with $95\%$ moving-block bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) over $60\text{ s}$ blocks. Every estimator uses standardized filter noise covariances, SMO gains, and neural dimensions documented in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}. For hardware-in-the-loop timing, \texttt{float32} C kernels are compiled using the ARM GNU Compiler Collection (GCC, CMSIS-DSP), with exact clock cycles ($N_{\text{cyc}}$) metered by the hardware Data Watchpoint and Trace cycle counter (\texttt{DWT->CYCCNT}) on an STM32F407VG (Cortex-M4, $168\text{ MHz}$) and clock-scaled to a Cortex-M7 ($480\text{ MHz}$).

\subsection{Standardized Automotive Dynamic Stress Cycles}
Four automotive duty cycles probe distinct electrochemical excitation bandwidths. Urban stop-and-go driving with frequent regenerative braking is captured by UDDS (concatenated to $3600\text{ s}$) and multi-rate FUDS profiles, which serve as the primary testbeds. Aggressive highway acceleration featuring sustained high-rate pulses up to $4\text{C}$ ($600\text{ s}$) is represented by US06, while USABC step-wise DST pulses provide standardized multi-amplitude validation.

\subsection{Quantitative Performance Indices}
Algorithmic fidelity is quantified over $N$ samples relative to reference trajectory $\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}}$ using RMSE, mean absolute error (MAE), maximum absolute error (MaxE), and first-entry reach time ($t_{\text{reach}}^{(2\%)}$) into the $2.0\%$ tolerance band from large initialization offsets ($\Delta\text{SOC}_0 = -40\%$):
\begin{align}
\text{RMSE} &= \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^N (\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_k)^2} \times 100\%, \label{eqn:rmse}\\
\text{MAE} &= \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^N |\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_k| \times 100\%, \label{eqn:mae}\\
\text{MaxE} &= \max_{1 \le k \le N} |\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_k| \times 100\%, \label{eqn:maxe}\\
t_{\text{reach}}^{(2\%)} &= \min\left\{ t \ge 0 \mid |\text{SOC}_t^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_t| \le 2.0\% \right\}. \label{eqn:treach}
\end{align}
Complementing first-entry reach time, permanent settling time $t_{\text{settle}}^{(5\%)}$ records the earliest time after which tracking remains continuously within the $5.0\%$ operational corridor through evaluation horizon $T_{\text{eval}}$, while peak post-convergence error $\text{MaxE}_{\text{dyn}}$ captures the worst-case transient excursion following $t_{\text{reach}}^{(2\%)}$. Finally, degradation robustness across aging is summarized by the \textbf{Aging Sensitivity Factor (ASF)}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:asf}
\text{ASF} = \frac{\text{RMSE}(\text{SOH}=80\%) - \text{RMSE}(\text{SOH}=100\%)}{\text{RMSE}(\text{SOH}=100\%)} \times 100\%.
\end{equation}

\subsection[Phase 2: Progressive Aging Degradation]{Phase 2: Progressive Aging Degradation ($100\% \rightarrow 80\%$ SOH)}

\subsection[Phase 3: Thermal Robustness Across Wide Environmental Regimes (0 to 45 deg C)]{Phase 3: Thermal Robustness Across Wide Environmental Regimes ($0^\circ\text{C}$ to $45^\circ\text{C}$)}

\subsection[Phase 6: Multi-Chemistry Generalization: LiFePO4 (LFP) Flat Plateau]{Phase 6: Multi-Chemistry Generalization: $\text{LiFePO}_4$ (LFP) Flat Plateau}
Figure~\ref{fig:lfp} and Table~\ref{tab:5_lfp} evaluate estimation performance on LFP chemistry, whose wide biphasic plateau ($20\%\text{--}80\%$ SOC) drives the open-circuit voltage gradient $d V_{\text{ocv}} / d\,\text{SOC}$ toward zero and sharply degrades voltage-to-SOC sensitivity. Consequently, standard EKF and AEKF experience severe error amplification across the plateau, while SMO suffers pronounced chattering once voltage slope regularization saturates.

Incorporating dynamic hysteresis transforms this observability landscape. Whereas a three-state model loses practical observability across the plateau, coupling the hysteresis state under bidirectional current reversals provides an effective switching slope across the loop that maintains a strictly positive-definite LTV observability Gramian, enabling DEKF to achieve sub-percent tracking as the strongest classical LFP estimator (conversely, purely unidirectional current saturates hysteresis and reverts toward open-loop integration). Equally important, because the capacity-sensitivity Jacobian in Eq.~\eqref{eqn:dekf_param_jacobian} scales linearly with $V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^+)$, $Q_{\text{act}}$ adaptation automatically freezes across the flat plateau and concentrates parameter updates strictly within the steep upper and lower knees, while rest intervals apply innovation gating ($\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} = \mathbf{0}$) to preserve stability. Meanwhile, standalone neural networks suffer substantial zero-shot transfer errors across chemistries due to unmodeled hysteresis, though chemistry-specific retraining restores accuracy below $2\%$ RMSE.

\subsection{Phase 8: Model-Based Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) Analysis Across Aging}
Figure~\ref{fig:eis} presents model-based Randles--CPE Nyquist spectra ($100\text{ kHz}\text{--}1\text{ mHz}$) parameterized from Oxford EIS aging ratios~\cite{Birkl2017diagnostics}. At angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$ ($\text{rad/s}$), complex electrochemical impedance $Z(j\omega)$ couples bulk ohmic and SEI resistance $R_0 = R_{\text{ohm}} + R_{\text{sei}}$ with charge-transfer resistance $R_{\text{ct}}$, Warburg diffusion impedance $Z_w(j\omega)$, CPE parameter $Q_{\text{cpe}}$, depression exponent $\alpha$, and Warburg diffusion coefficient $\sigma_w$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:eis_randles}
Z(j\omega) = R_0 + \frac{R_{\text{ct}} + Z_w(j\omega)}{1 + (j\omega)^\alpha Q_{\text{cpe}} \left(R_{\text{ct}} + Z_w(j\omega)\right)}, \qquad Z_w(j\omega) = \sigma_w \omega^{-1/2}(1 - j).
\end{equation}
Across aging down to $80\%$ SOH (Table~\ref{tab:ecm_params}), ohmic resistance $R_0$ expands by $50\%$, whereas charge-transfer resistance $R_{\text{ct}}$ widens by $150\%$. Viewed through the Laplace-domain 2-RC impedance (Laplace variable $s$), this differential shift between aged and nominal branch dynamics generates an unmodeled kinetic polarization residual $\Delta V_{\text{pol}}(t)$ proportional to sustained load current. Uncompensated EKF misinterprets this expanded polarization voltage as an OCV shift, precipitating its aging divergence; standard DEKF absorbs $R_0$ growth online but omits $R_1$ to avoid branch collinearity, leaving $\Delta V_{\text{pol}}(t)$ to be compensated directly by the PIDKF neural module.

\subsection{Phase 9: Module and Pack-Level Scaling (8S Series Pack)}

\section{Industrial BMS Implementation Guidelines}
The Pareto frontier identified across our ten-phase benchmark yields direct engineering guidance for selecting estimators across industrial battery applications. For passenger electric vehicles (EVs) and multi-chemistry traction packs that experience wide temperature swings and multi-year capacity fade, the proposed hybrid \textbf{PIDKF} provides the strongest accuracy--compute trade-off across both NMC and LFP chemistries, whereas uncompensated EKF should be avoided because of severe aging divergence. In stationary LFP energy-storage installations operating predominantly along the flat biphasic plateau, deploying DEKF augmented with dynamic hysteresis co-estimation maintains sub-percent plateau accuracy through open-loop Coulomb scaling without requiring auxiliary mechanical strain sensors.

In environments plagued by simultaneous Hall-effect current drift and power-inverter EMI, control architects face a clear design choice between noise suppression and bias rejection: Kalman architectures (EKF and DEKF) attenuate high-frequency voltage ripple to minimize instantaneous tracking error, whereas SMO clamps long-term DC current offset via sliding-manifold switching at the expense of higher switching ripple. Finally, for cloud--edge fleet architectures, running lightweight DEKF or PIDKF kernels directly on automotive microcontrollers ($14.20\;\upmu\text{s}$ per step on Cortex-M4) keeps real-time safety loops strictly deterministic while offloading heavy neural retraining and fleet-wide health analytics to the cloud.

\section{Conclusions}
Through a unified dual-tier evaluation spanning six representative estimators and our proposed hybrid PIDKF across ten operational stress phases, this benchmark establishes why laboratory room-temperature rankings break down in deployed BMSs. First, progressive battery aging down to $80\%$ SOH exposes a fundamental vulnerability in fixed-parameter filters: standard EKF suffers a $45.7$-fold RMSE surge as unmodeled impedance growth---driven primarily by a $>150\%$ expansion in charge-transfer resistance $R_{\text{ct}}$ revealed by Randles--CPE Nyquist analysis---is misattributed to open-circuit potential shifts, whereas online parameter-tracking DEKF reduces aged degradation error by nearly an order of magnitude. Second, chemistry and spatial topology govern observability: augmenting DEKF with dynamic hysteresis co-estimation restores sub-percent plateau tracking on biphasic LFP cells, while distributed per-cell estimation across an 8S pack eliminates weakest-cell overdischarge divergence caused by interior thermal gradients relative to lumped string observers. Third, pairing DEKF with dynamic hysteresis and a Butler--Volmer-constrained neural overpotential compensator yields complementary gains---neural residual learning dominates on aged sloped NMC while hysteresis tracking dominates on aged flat-plateau LFP---maintaining sub-$0.5\%$ experimental CALCE aged RMSE across both chemistries while exogenous Jacobian decoupling holds bare-metal Cortex-M4 execution to $14.20\;\upmu\text{s}$ per step.

Several limitations motivate future investigation. Extending the coupled electro-thermal-aging framework to large-format prismatic and pouch cells with spatial lithium-plating gradients will test scalability beyond cylindrical formats. Broadening empirical validation across larger manufacturing cohorts beyond the current two-cell batch splits will sharpen statistical variance bounds, while coupling online impedance estimation with end-of-life knee-point models below $70\%$ SOH will support second-life grid storage qualification.

\section{State-Space Matrices and Analytic Jacobians\label{app:jacobians}}
For the three-state NMC and four-state LFP models, the discrete transition, observation, excitation, and parameter-sensitivity operators take the explicit block-structured forms:
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{A}_k^{\text{NMC}} &= \operatorname{diag}(1, a_1, a_2), &
\mathbf{A}_k^{\text{LFP}} &= \operatorname{diag}(1, a_1, a_2, a_{h,k}), \\
\mathbf{H}_k^{\text{NMC}} &= \begin{bmatrix} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^-) & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, &
\mathbf{H}_k^{\text{LFP}} &= \begin{bmatrix} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^-) & -1 & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \\
\mathbf{B}_k &= \begin{bmatrix} B_{1,k} & B_{2,k} & B_{3,k} & (0) \end{bmatrix}^T, &
\mathbf{C}_{\theta,k} &= \begin{bmatrix} -I_k & -\dfrac{\eta \Delta t I_{k-1}}{3600} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^+) \end{bmatrix},
\end{align*}
\begin{equation*}
\mathbf{g}_k = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & (1 - a_{h,k}) M(z_k)\operatorname{sgn}(I_k) \end{bmatrix}^T,
\end{equation*}
with discrete decay poles $a_i = e^{-\Delta t/\tau_i}$ and $a_{h,k} = e^{-\beta|\Delta z_k|}$. Locally over short frozen-coefficient windows ($a_{h,k} \approx a_h$), the observability determinant satisfies $|\det(\mathcal{O}_{n,k})| = V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k) \prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(1 - a_i) \prod_{i < j} |a_i - a_j|$; full rank requires $V_{\text{ocv}}' > 0$, $I_k \ne 0$, and pole non-resonance $a_{h,k} \ne a_1, a_2$. On the LFP plateau, constant saturation $M(z) \equiv M_0 = 30\text{ mV}$ makes $\mathbf{A}_k^{\text{LFP}}$ block-diagonal and yields Eq.~\eqref{eqn:det_obs_lfp}.

Evaluated across active $N=30\text{ s}$ persistent excitation windows ($I_{\text{RMS}} \ge 0.5\text{ A}$) under state normalization $\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = [z, U_1/0.1, U_2/0.1, V_h/0.03]^T$ across $3,698$ total windows---comprising UDDS ($M_w = 1,370$), US06 ($M_w = 596$), FUDS ($M_w = 1,372$), and DST ($M_w = 360$, where $M_w$ denotes window count) across $80\%\text{--}100\%$ SOH---the minimum LTV Gramian eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{G}_o) \ge 1.42 \times 10^{-3}$ (UDDS), $\ge 1.88 \times 10^{-3}$ (US06), $\ge 1.35 \times 10^{-3}$ (FUDS), and $\ge 1.21 \times 10^{-3}$ (DST) at $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($\ge 1.18 \times 10^{-3}$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$; $\ge 1.55 \times 10^{-3}$ at $45^\circ\text{C}$), confirming uniform complete observability under sub-$1\text{ s}$ ZOH dwell at singleton resonance currents.

\section{Discrete Quasi-Sliding Mode Stability and UUB of SMO\label{app:lyapunov}}
Along sliding manifold $S_k = V_{\text{meas},k} - \hat{V}_{t,k}$, the one-step manifold error evolves as:
\begin{align*}
S_{k+1} &= S_k + \Delta f_k - \gamma(\hat{z}_k)\operatorname{sat}\!\left(\frac{S_k}{\mu}\right), \\
\Delta f_k &= -R_0\Delta I_k + \left[V_{\text{ocv}}'(\xi_{k+1}) - V_{\text{ocv}}'(\xi_k)\right] e_{z,k} - V_{\text{ocv}}'\Delta Q_k \\
&\quad + \sum_{i=1}^2 \left[(1-a_i)e_{U_i,k} - d_{U_i,k}\right] + \left[(1-a_{h,k})e_{h,k} - \Delta M_k\right] + (v_{k+1} - v_k),
\end{align*}
with scheduled gain $\gamma(\hat{z}) \equiv \mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}) = l_{\text{smo}}$ whenever $V_{\text{ocv}}' \ge \alpha_{\min}$, current increment $\Delta I_k = I_{k+1} - I_k$, intermediate OCV evaluation points $\xi_k, \xi_{k+1}$, state estimation errors $e_{z,k}, e_{U_i,k}, e_{h,k}$, branch overpotential and hysteresis perturbations $d_{U_i,k}, \Delta M_k$, bounded capacity mismatch term $V_{\text{ocv}}'\Delta Q_k \le 0.35\text{ mV}$, and bounded analog front-end (AFE) measurement noise $|v_k| \le V_{\text{AFE}} \le 0.84\text{ mV}$ ($\pm 0.02\%$ F.S.; distinct from filter $R_v$).

Across nominal cycles ($\le 2.5\text{C}$ NMC, $\le 2\text{C}$ LFP), summing ohmic step, OCV curvature, capacity mismatch, RC decay, and front-end noise bounds gives $|\Delta f_k^{\text{NMC}}| \le 4.18\text{ mV}$ (with $\bar{e}_h = 0$) and $|\Delta f_k^{\text{LFP}}| \le 4.82\text{ mV}$ (with $\bar{e}_h = 2M_0 = 60\text{ mV}$), both within nominal sliding bound $\Phi_d \equiv 5\text{ mV}$. Even under $4\text{C}$ pulses ($|\Delta f_k| \le 6.16\text{ mV} < 2\mu - l_{\text{smo}} = 15\text{ mV}$), strict reaching margin $\eta_{\text{reach},4\text{C}} = 8.84\text{ mV}$ and invariant radius $r_{\text{ult},4\text{C}} = 8.21\text{ mV} < \mu$ hold (while Phase~7 faults with $I_{\text{bias}} = 0.25\text{ A}$ and $25\text{ mV}$ EMI exceed $\Phi_d$, producing graceful degradation). Choosing quadratic Lyapunov candidate $V_k = \tfrac{1}{2} S_k^2$ yields the following stability guarantees:

\emph{Contraction and Invariance}: Outside boundary layer $\mathcal{B}_\mu \equiv \{|S| \le \mu\}$, satisfying $\Phi_d < l_{\text{smo}} < 2\mu - \Phi_d$ guarantees minimum reaching decrement $\eta_{\text{reach}} = 0.010\text{ V}$ per step ($|S_k| - |S_{k+1}| \ge \eta_{\text{reach}}$), driving trajectories into $\mathcal{B}_\mu$ in finite step bound $k^* \le \lceil (|S_0| - \mu)/\eta_{\text{reach}} \rceil$. Inside $\mathcal{B}_\mu$, contraction ratio $|1 - l_{\text{smo}}/\mu| = 0.25 < 1$ makes the ultimate band $\Omega_{\text{ult}} \equiv \{|S| \le 6.67\text{ mV}\} \subset \mathcal{B}_\mu$ positively invariant.

\emph{Uniform Ultimate Boundedness (UUB)}: Branch perturbations $|d_{U_i,k}| \le (1-a_i)\tilde{D}_{U_i}$ with $\tilde{D}_{U_i} \le 3.5\text{ mV}$ across $0^\circ\text{C}\text{--}45^\circ\text{C}$ ensure passive ISS bounds $|e_{U_i}| \le \bar{e}_{U_i} \le 3.5\text{ mV}$ ($l_2 = l_3 = 0$), while LFP hysteresis remains bounded by $|e_h| \le \bar{e}_h = 60\text{ mV}$ ($l_h = 0$). Applying the mean value theorem (MVT) yields $|e_{z,k}| \le (\mu + 2\bar{e}_U + \bar{e}_h + V_{\text{AFE}})/\alpha$ (with $\bar{e}_U = \max_i \bar{e}_{U_i}$), bounding NMC SOC error tightly ($|e_z| \le 7.2\%$ across all $T$ and SOH; $\le 6.4\%$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$) and ensuring finite UUB ($\|\mathbf{e}_x\| < \infty$ for state error vector $\mathbf{e}_x$; analytical asymptotic limit $|e_z| \le 31.94$ as $V_{\text{ocv}}' \to \alpha_{\text{LFP}}$) on LFP.

All filters share identical initialization and embedded ARM GCC 12.3 (\texttt{-O3}, CMSIS-DSP v5.9.0) compilation, with clock frequency $f_{\text{clk}}$ scaled via $t = N_{\text{cyc}}/f_{\text{clk}}$ from STM32F407VG ($168\text{ MHz}$) to Cortex-M7 ($480\text{ MHz}$): EKF ($\mathbf{P}_0 = \operatorname{diag}(10^{-2}, 10^{-4}, 10^{-4})$, $\mathbf{Q} = \operatorname{diag}(10^{-6}, 10^{-5}, 10^{-5})$, $R_v = 10^{-3}\text{ V}^2$); UKF ($\alpha_{\text{UT}} = 10^{-3}$, $\beta_{\text{UT}} = 2$, $\kappa_{\text{UT}} = 0$, $7$ sigma points); AEKF ($\lambda_{\text{SH}} = 0.985$, $d_k = (1 - \lambda_{\text{SH}})/(1 - \lambda_{\text{SH}}^k)$, $R_{v,\min} = 10^{-4}\text{ V}^2$); DEKF ($\mathbf{P}_{\theta,0} = \operatorname{diag}(10^{-4}, 10^{-4})$, $\mathbf{Q}_\theta = \operatorname{diag}(10^{-8}, 10^{-9})$, $R_{\theta,v} = 10^{-2}\text{ V}^2$); SMO ($l_{\text{smo}} = 0.025\text{ V}$, $\mu = 0.02\text{ V}$, $\alpha_{\min} = 0.40/0.00275\text{ V}$ for NMC/LFP); BiLSTM-Attn ($W = 30\text{ s}$, 1 layer, $D = 32$, $24,500$ parameters, $98\text{ KB}$ Flash); PINN-SOC (4-layer $[64, 64, 32]$ GELU MLP, AdamW, 5 seeds, $\lambda_{\text{Faraday}} = 0.40$, $\lambda_{\text{KVL}} = 0.30$, $\lambda_{\text{mono}} = 0.10$); and PIDKF (2-hidden-layer $[16, 8]$ GELU MLP, $257$ parameters, $1.03\text{ KB}$ Flash, $560$ FLOPs/step, AdamW, 200 epochs, $\sigma_{\text{NN}} = 5.8\text{ mV}$, $\sigma_{\text{approx}} = 1.9\text{ mV}$, $R_{\text{eff},k} = R_v + \sigma_{\text{NN}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{approx}}^2 = 1.04 \times 10^{-3}\text{ V}^2$, $R_{\theta,\text{eff}} = R_{\theta,v} + \sigma_{\text{NN}}^2$, hold-out bound $\bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{emp}} = 20.0\text{ mV}$, rest gating threshold $I_{\text{rest,th}} = 10\text{ mA}$, max rest $T_{\max} = 120\text{ s}$, exogenous Jacobian discrepancy $<3.8\%$ on $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}_k$ and $<0.12\%$ on $\Delta\mathbf{K}_\theta$).

For discrete-time Riccati invariance and multi-cycle PIDKF contraction, let $\boldsymbol{\Phi}_F(k, j) \equiv \prod_{l=j}^{k-1}\mathbf{F}_l$ denote the closed-loop error transition matrix over $k > j$ with uniform norm bound $\bar{F} \ge \sup_k \|\mathbf{F}_k\|$. Each active--rest cycle of length $L_m = N + T_{\text{rest},m} \le N + T_{\max}$ ($N = 30\text{ s}$) contracts by $\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}_F(k_m + L_m, k_m)\| \le \rho_{\text{PE}} < 1$. Partitioning $[j, k]$ into $M = \lfloor (k - j)/(N + T_{\max}) \rfloor$ full cycles and partial boundary segments gives uniform exponential contraction alongside Joseph-stabilized Riccati covariance and gain bounds under UCO ($\mathcal{G}_o \succeq \alpha_o \mathbf{I}$), upper plant norm $\bar{a} \equiv \sup_k \|\mathbf{A}_k\|_2$, $\underline{q}\mathbf{I} \preceq \mathbf{Q}_k \preceq \bar{q}\mathbf{I}$, and $0 < \underline{r} \le R_{\text{eff},k} \le \bar{r}$:
\begin{align*}
\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}_F(k, j)\| &\le c\rho^{k-j}, &
\rho &\equiv \rho_{\text{PE}}^{1/(N + T_{\max})} < 1, \qquad c \equiv \bar{F}^{N + T_{\max}} \rho^{-(N + T_{\max})} \ge 1, \\
\underline{p}\mathbf{I} &\preceq \mathbf{P}_k^+ \preceq \bar{p}\mathbf{I}, &
\underline{p} &= \underline{q}, \qquad \bar{p} = (\alpha_o^{-1} + N\bar{q})\bar{a}^{2N}, \qquad \|\mathbf{K}_k\|_2 \le \frac{\bar{p}\|\mathbf{H}_k\|_2}{\underline{r}} \le \bar{K} < \infty,
\end{align*}
validating Eqs.~\eqref{eqn:bibs_pidkf}--\eqref{eqn:ms_pidkf}.
