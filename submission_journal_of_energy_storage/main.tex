\begin{abstract}
Electrified transportation and grid storage rely on lithium-ion battery management systems (BMS), where state-of-charge (SOC) estimation governs safety and power control. In field service, however, aging down to $80\%$ state-of-health (SOH), thermal extremes ($0^\circ\text{C}$ to $45^\circ\text{C}$), flat lithium iron phosphate (LFP) voltage plateaus, and pack inconsistency distort impedance and weaken observability. Because existing benchmarks predominantly test fresh cells at room temperature, how observers and neural networks degrade across these coupled stressors without oracle priors, and how they fit microcontroller budgets, remains unclear. We establish a dual-tier benchmark spanning public cycler datasets and ten-phase co-simulation, and propose a Physics-Informed Dual Kalman Filter (PIDKF) fusing online dual parameter tracking, dynamic hysteresis cancellation, and a $257$-parameter Butler--Volmer compensator. Whereas fixed-parameter Extended Kalman Filters (EKF) exhibit a $55.9$-fold root-mean-square error (RMSE) surge ($0.067\%$ to $3.745\%$) at $80\%$ SOH and standalone neural networks drift out of distribution, PIDKF limits $80\%$ SOH RMSE to $0.928\pm0.027\%$ and generalizes across held-out cycler cells ($0.636\%$ to $2.750\%$) and series packs ($0.544\%$ weakest-cell RMSE). On a $168\;\mathrm{MHz}$ Advanced RISC Machines (ARM) Cortex-M4F microcontroller, PIDKF executes in $17.74\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$ per step using $364\;\mathrm{B}$ random-access memory (RAM) and $7.25\;\mathrm{KB}$ Flash.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The global transition toward electrified transportation and renewable grid storage relies heavily on high-energy-density lithium-ion battery (LIB) packs~\cite{Tarascon2001,Lu2013review}. Within automotive battery management systems (BMS), the State-of-Charge (SOC), defined as the ratio of remaining usable charge to calibrated capacity, serves as the core variable for driving-range prediction, regenerative braking allocation, over-charge and over-discharge protection, and State-of-Power (SOP) enveloping~\cite{Plett2004part1,Xiong2018joint,Farmann2016peukert}. Unlike terminal voltage or surface temperature, internal lithium stoichiometry cannot be measured directly during operation because solid-state diffusion, interfacial charge transfer, and electrode phase transitions remain coupled~\cite{Doyle1993,Torchio2016pybam}. Open-loop Coulomb counting offers a constant-time $\mathcal{O}(1)$ baseline~\cite{Plett2004part1} but drifts unboundedly under current-sensor offset, analog-to-digital converter (ADC) quantization, and side reactions. Conversely, open-circuit voltage (OCV) lookup requires $30\text{--}120\text{ min}$ of zero-current relaxation to extinguish diffusion overpotentials~\cite{Roscher2011hysteresis,Verbrugge2004hysteresis}. Reliable real-time SOC tracking therefore depends on closed-loop observers or learning models that infer internal states from noisy terminal telemetry~\cite{Plett2004part2,Hu2012ecm,Ren2019aging}.

To balance physical interpretability with real-time tractability, model-based observers typically pair recursive filtering with lumped equivalent circuit models (ECMs). Common circuit topologies include first-order resistor--capacitor (1-RC) Thevenin, hybrid circuit-capacity, and second-order (2-RC) dual-polarization networks~\cite{He2011ecm,Hu2012ecm,Kim2011}. The industry-standard Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) linearizes the nonlinear OCV--SOC curve via first-order Taylor expansion~\cite{Plett2004part3,Santhanagopalan2006jes}, whereas the Adaptive EKF (AEKF) updates noise covariances online through Sage--Husa recursions~\cite{Xiang2022aekf}. Higher-order sigma-point variants, such as the Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) and Cubature Kalman Filter (CKF), propagate deterministic sample points to curb linearization error~\cite{Plett2006ukf,He2013ukf,Li2021ackf}. To track lifetime parameter drift, the Dual EKF (DEKF) co-estimates dynamic states alongside effective series resistance $R_0$ and actual usable capacity $Q_{\text{act}}$~\cite{Charkhgard2010dekf,Yang2024degradation}, while Sliding Mode Observers (SMO) force voltage residuals onto a switching manifold for bounded disturbance rejection~\cite{Chen2016smo,Huangfu2018smo}. Continuum formulations, spanning the pseudo-two-dimensional (P2D) Doyle--Fuller--Newman model~\cite{Doyle1993,Torchio2016pybam,Northrop2014p2d}, the Single Particle Model (SPM), and the electrolyte-extended SPM (SPMe), support partial differential equation (PDE) observers~\cite{DiDomenico2010spm,Moura2017pde,Marquis2019spme}, yet spatial discretization remains too costly for low-cost automotive processors.

Data-driven machine learning approaches bypass explicit circuit modeling by learning sequence-to-state mappings from voltage, current, and temperature histories. Representative methods include support vector regression, Gaussian processes, Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM), Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), Temporal Convolutional Networks (TCN), and self-attention Transformers~\cite{Hansen2005svr,Chemali2018dnn,Liu2020gpr,Song2019bilstm,Jiao2020attn,Liu2021tcn,Vaswani2017attention}. Because purely black-box networks lack physical conservation invariants, they often extrapolate poorly under unseen thermal or aging shifts. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) address this limitation by embedding Faradaic charge conservation, Kirchhoff's voltage law (KVL), and thermodynamic monotonicity into training via automatic differentiation over collocation domains~\cite{Raissi2019pinn,Wang2024xjtu,Abbasi2025pinn_jes,Zhou2025dualstream_jes,Brancato2026}, alongside data-driven multi-cycle capacity prognostics~\cite{Li2021oneshot}. To combine recursive stability with nonlinear residual modeling, recent hybrid observers couple neural compensators or sequence models with Kalman filters~\cite{Charkhgard2010dekf,Tu2023hybrid,Wang2024aekf_lstm,Shen2025kf,Yu2025lstm_ekf}.

Despite rapid progress across model-based, data-driven, and hybrid estimators, four critical gaps persist between idealized literature benchmarks and deployed BMS operation:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Idealized test priors and coupled Arrhenius--aging cross-coupling:} Most benchmarks test fresh cells at $100\%$ state-of-health (SOH) near $25^\circ\text{C}$~\cite{Hu2012ecm,Chemali2018dnn,Le2026charging} or leak calibrated health updates during evaluation. This practice hides how solid-electrolyte interphase (SEI) growth down to $80\%$ SOH and Arrhenius kinetics across $0^\circ\text{C}\text{--}45^\circ\text{C}$~\cite{He2011ecm,Ren2019aging} inflate charge-transfer resistance $R_{\text{ct}}$ and distort OCV projections.
\item \emph{Absence of Jacobian decoupling, passivity bounds, and closed-loop stability in hybrid observers:} Prior neural--Kalman hybrids~\cite{Charkhgard2010dekf,Tu2023hybrid,Wang2024aekf_lstm,Shen2025kf,Yu2025lstm_ekf} rarely analyze how noisy automatic-differentiation neural Jacobians destabilize the Kalman gain. They also seldom enforce bounded Butler--Volmer dissipation or prove discrete-time stability across drive--rest duty cycles.
\item \emph{Cross-chemistry observability collapse on biphasic cathodes and pack dispersion:} Rankings obtained on steep-sloped lithium nickel manganese cobalt oxide (NMC) cells do not transfer directly to lithium iron phosphate ($\text{LiFePO}_4$, LFP) cells. Along the flat LFP plateau (voltage gradient $d\text{OCV}/d\text{SOC} \approx 0$), dynamic hysteresis masks observability~\cite{Roscher2011hysteresis,Verbrugge2004hysteresis,Marongiu2016hyst} unless auxiliary expansion-force sensors are added~\cite{Tian2025}. Hall-effect offset drift, pulse-width modulation (PWM) electromagnetic interference (EMI)~\cite{Hashemi2020}, and cell-to-cell thermal-aging spread~\cite{Yang2024degradation,Zheng2018inconsistency} further stress series packs.
\item \emph{Unquantified embedded microcontroller feasibility:} Deep sequence and dual-stream PINN architectures~\cite{Song2019bilstm,Bhattacharya2024gpr,Zhou2025dualstream_jes} are rarely audited for random-access memory (RAM), Flash, or execution latency on automotive microcontroller units (MCUs) such as Advanced RISC Machines (ARM) Cortex-M4F and M7 cores.
\end{enumerate}

To close these gaps, this paper builds a unified dual-tier benchmark under strict blind fresh room-temperature prior initialization. In real deployments, the BMS boots from factory fresh-cell parameters ($100\%$ SOH, $25^\circ\text{C}$) without an external SOH oracle. Tier~1 therefore tests real laboratory aging and thermal cells from Xi'an Jiaotong University (XJTU) Batch-5 dynamic Random Walk (RW) cycles~\cite{Wang2024xjtu} and the National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Ames Prognostics Center of Excellence (PCoE) aging and electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) repository~\cite{Saha2007nasa}. Complementing Tier~1, Tier~2 isolates individual degradation, thermal, hysteresis, sensor, and pack stressors against exact calibrated ground truth across four driving profiles: the Urban Dynamometer Driving Schedule (UDDS), the Supplemental Federal Test Procedure (US06), the Dynamic Stress Test (DST), and the Federal Urban Driving Schedule (FUDS). Across both tiers, we benchmark EKF, AEKF, UKF, DEKF, SMO, attention-augmented BiLSTM (BiLSTM-Attn), an unconstrained multi-layer perceptron (MLP), PINN-based SOC estimation (PINN-SOC), and a newly proposed hybrid Physics-Informed Dual Kalman Filter (PIDKF). Relative to prior physics-informed and neural--Kalman studies, the principal contributions of this work are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exogenously decoupled hybrid PIDKF with provable stability:} PIDKF pairs a DEKF backbone (co-estimating online series resistance and inverse capacity) with a $257$-parameter compensator conditioned on rapid online \emph{a priori} series-resistance adaptation and bounded by Butler--Volmer passivity. Shielding the state Kalman gain from neural derivatives via \emph{exogenous sensitivity decoupling} avoids the covariance instability of coupled chain-rule hybrids~\cite{Charkhgard2010dekf,Wang2024aekf_lstm,Yu2025lstm_ekf} and establishes discrete Input-to-State Stability (ISS) and Uniformly Ultimately Bounded (UUB) convergence.
\item \textbf{Analytical LFP hysteresis bias-cancellation theory and 8S pack scaling:} Closed-form four-state determinants and $30\;\mathrm{s}$ linear time-varying (LTV) observability Gramians (minimum eigenvalue $\ge 1.09\times 10^{-7}$ on UDDS) prove that dynamic hysteresis tracking improves LFP precision via systematic innovation bias cancellation rather than matrix conditioning. This formulation lowers fresh LFP tracking error nearly eightfold ($0.401\% \rightarrow 0.049\text{--}0.051\%$) and scales across distributed eight-cell series (8S) packs to $0.544\%$ weakest-cell root-mean-square error (RMSE).
\item \textbf{Ten-phase zero-leakage dual-tier diagnosis of degradation and sensor stress:} We quantify aging resilience via an Aging Sensitivity Factor (ASF) and fit Randles constant-phase-element (Randles--CPE) EIS spectra on real NASA \texttt{B0006} data ($R_{\text{ct}}: 22.37 \rightarrow 38.89\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$), explaining EKF's $55.9$-fold RMSE surge ($0.067\% \rightarrow 3.745\%$) under blind $100\%$ SOH / $25^\circ\text{C}$ priors. Under large $-40\%$ initial SOC offsets and $2$-hour EMI/bias faults, PIDKF keeps $80\%$ SOH RMSE at $0.928\pm0.027\%$, reaches $1.425\text{--}2.750\%$ on held-out XJTU cells and $0.636\text{--}1.715\%$ on NASA \texttt{B0007}, and cuts $4^\circ\text{C}$ cold NASA error by $63\%$.
\item \textbf{Deterministic 32-bit ARM Cortex-M sizing with static C kernels:} We export bit-consistent PyTorch weights into zero-heap American National Standards Institute C99 (ANSI-C99) kernels and audit Flash, RAM, latency, and central processing unit (CPU) load via analytical 32-bit ARM Cortex-M instruction-set (ARMv7E-M) accounting. PIDKF executes in $17.74\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$ per step with $364\;\mathrm{B}$ RAM, $7.25\;\mathrm{KB}$ Flash, and $0.28\%$ CPU load for $16$ cells at $10\;\mathrm{Hz}$.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of this paper formulates the coupled electro-thermal-aging battery model and LFP observability theory in Section~2, details the benchmark estimators and proposed hybrid PIDKF in Section~3, and describes the dual-tier experimental and co-simulation platform in Section~4. Section~5 presents the ten-phase comparative results, Section~6 distills industrial BMS selection guidelines, Section~7 summarizes the main conclusions, and Appendices~A--B compile discrete SMO stability proofs, calibrated model constants, and Riccati invariance bounds.

\section{Electrochemical Battery Modeling and Degradation Dynamics}
This section formulates the coupled electro-thermal and aging dynamics of NMC and LFP cells used throughout our benchmark. We first establish the discrete-time equivalent circuit and Arrhenius thermal balance, then define the structural parameter-allocation boundary separating the aging plant from the observers, and finally analyze OCV--SOC monotonicity, dynamic hysteresis, and flat-plateau observability under persistent excitation.

\subsection{Second-Order Dual-Polarization (2-RC) Model Formulation}
The electrical response of a lithium-ion cell is modeled using a second-order Dual Polarization equivalent circuit, widely recognized as an effective compromise between physical fidelity and real-time tractability~\cite{Hu2012ecm,Xiong2014}. The circuit couples an open-circuit voltage source $V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC})$ in series with an effective ohmic series resistance $R_0 = R_{\text{ohm}} + R_{\text{sei}}$ (combining metallic ohmic resistance $R_{\text{ohm}}$ and SEI film resistance $R_{\text{sei}}$) and two parallel resistor--capacitor ($R_i \parallel C_i$, $i \in \{1, 2\}$) branches representing fast interfacial charge-transfer kinetics and slower solid-state lithium diffusion, respectively:
\begin{align}
\frac{d\,\text{SOC}(t)}{dt} &= -\frac{\eta(I)}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}}\,I(t), \label{eqn:soc_diff}\\
\frac{d U_1(t)}{dt} &= -\frac{U_1(t)}{R_1 C_1} + \frac{I(t)}{C_1}, \quad \frac{d U_2(t)}{dt} = -\frac{U_2(t)}{R_2 C_2} + \frac{I(t)}{C_2}, \label{eqn:u12_diff}
\end{align}
where $t$ is continuous time ($\text{s}$), $I(t)$ is load current (positive during discharge), $U_1(t)$ and $U_2(t)$ are the fast activation and slow concentration polarization overpotentials ($\text{V}$) across branch resistances $R_1, R_2$ ($\Omega$) and capacitances $C_1, C_2$ ($\text{F}$). Here, $\eta(I)$ (abbreviated $\eta$) is the asymmetric Faradaic coulombic efficiency ($\eta = 1.000$ under discharge $I \ge 0$ and $\eta = 0.995$ under charge $I < 0$), and $Q_{\text{act}}$ is the usable capacity ($\text{A}\cdot\text{h}$). By Kirchhoff's voltage law, terminal voltage $V_t(t)$ obeys:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:vt_diff}
V_t(t) = V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC}(t)) - U_1(t) - U_2(t) - I(t) R_0,
\end{equation}
so resistive and polarization voltage drops strictly depress terminal voltage under discharge while guaranteeing nonnegative irreversible entropy production in accordance with the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

Discretizing Eqs.~\eqref{eqn:soc_diff}--\eqref{eqn:u12_diff} at sampling interval $\Delta t = 1.0\text{ s}$ and step index $k$ via zero-order hold (ZOH) on the piecewise-constant current $I_k$ yields the discrete-time state-space representation with polarization time constants $\tau_i = R_i C_i$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:state_space_discrete}
\mathbf{x}_{k+1} = \mathbf{A}_k \mathbf{x}_k + \mathbf{B}_k I_k + \mathbf{w}_k,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_k = [\text{SOC}_k, U_{1,k}, U_{2,k}]^T$ is the discrete state vector (with superscript $T$ denoting matrix transpose) and $\mathbf{w}_k \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}_k)$ denotes a multivariate Gaussian process noise vector with zero mean $\mathbf{0}$ and covariance matrix $\mathbf{Q}_k$. The diagonal state-transition matrix $\mathbf{A}_k$ (where $\operatorname{diag}(\cdot)$ constructs a diagonal matrix) and input vector $\mathbf{B}_k$ are:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{A}_k &= \operatorname{diag}\left(1, e^{-\Delta t/\tau_1}, e^{-\Delta t/\tau_2}\right), \label{eqn:Ak_mat} \\
\mathbf{B}_k &= \begin{bmatrix}
-\frac{\eta \Delta t}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}}, & R_1\left(1 - e^{-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_1}}\right), & R_2\left(1 - e^{-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_2}}\right)
\end{bmatrix}^T, \label{eqn:Bk_mat}
\end{align}
with nominal parameters calibrated in Table~\ref{tab:ecm_params} (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}). The corresponding nonlinear voltage measurement model is:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:measurement_eq}
y_k = V_k = V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC}_k) - U_{1,k} - U_{2,k} - I_k R_0 + v_k,
\end{equation}
where $y_k = V_k$ is the sampled terminal voltage and $v_k \sim \mathcal{N}(0, R_v)$ denotes zero-mean Gaussian measurement noise with scalar variance $R_v$.

\subsection{Dynamic Electro-Thermal Balance and Arrhenius Kinetics}
To capture temperature-dependent kinetics, cell core temperature $T(t)$ evolves according to a lumped thermal energy balance:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:thermal_balance}
C_{\text{th}} \frac{d T(t)}{dt} = \dot{Q}_{\text{gen}}(t) - \frac{T(t) - T_{\text{amb}}}{R_{\text{th}}},
\end{equation}
where $C_{\text{th}}$ is the lumped thermal capacitance, $R_{\text{th}}$ is the equivalent thermal resistance to ambient temperature $T_{\text{amb}}$, and total heat generation $\dot{Q}_{\text{gen}}(t)$ sums irreversible Joule and polarization losses with reversible entropic heat driven by the entropic coefficient $\partial V_{\text{ocv}}/\partial T$. Because potentiometric titration shows a predominantly negative entropic slope across normalized state-of-charge $z \equiv \text{SOC}$, reversible heating remains exothermic across most of discharge and reverses sign only near full charge in NMC cells. For both cylindrical cell geometries studied here, a small Biot number ($\text{Bi} \ll 1$) indicates spatially uniform internal heat conduction. Core-to-surface temperature gradients therefore stay minimal even under sustained $2\text{C}$ discharge, where an $n\text{C}$ rate equals $n$ times the nominal-capacity current $Q_{\text{init}}$ in amperes, justifying a single-lump core temperature linked to surface temperature $T_s$ through conductive resistance $R_{\text{cond}}$.

Internal resistances vary strongly with temperature via piecewise Arrhenius kinetics characterized by cold- and warm-regime activation energies $E_{a,i}^{\text{cold}}$ and $E_{a,i}^{\text{hot}}$ (reflecting lithium-ion desolvation barriers at sub-ambient temperatures), referenced to baseline resistances $R_{0,\text{ref}}$ and $R_{i,\text{ref}}$ at reference temperature $T_{\text{ref}}$ using the universal gas constant $R$:
\begin{equation}
R_0(T) = R_{0,\text{ref}} f_{T,0}(T), \qquad R_i(T) = R_{i,\text{ref}} f_{T,i}(T) \;\; (i \in \{1, 2\}), \label{eqn:arrh_ri}
\end{equation}
where $R_0(T)$ and $R_i(T)$ evolve dynamically online alongside internal core temperature $T(t)$ via dimensionless Arrhenius thermal scaling factors $f_{T,0}(T)$ and $f_{T,i}(T)$ (baseline values in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}).

\subsection{Semi-Empirical Aging Parameterization Across Degradation Regimes}
Although cell aging originates from complex microscopic mechanisms including SEI growth and active particle cracking~\cite{Birkl2017diagnostics,Ren2019aging}, real-time estimation relies on a separable macroscopic parameterization anchored to state-of-health $\text{SOH} \equiv Q_{\text{act}}(T_{\text{ref}})/Q_{\text{init}}$ relative to nominal fresh-cell resistances $R_{i,\text{nom}}$ and capacitances $C_{i,\text{nom}}$:
\begin{align}
R_0(T, \text{SOH}) &= R_{0,\text{nom}} f_{T,0}(T) [1.0 + \kappa_{R_0}\,(1.0 - \text{SOH})], &
R_1(T, \text{SOH}) &= R_{1,\text{nom}} f_{T,1}(T) [1.0 + \kappa_{R_1}\,(1.0 - \text{SOH})], \label{eqn:aging_r0}\\
R_2(T, \text{SOH}) &= R_{2,\text{nom}} f_{T,2}(T) [1.0 + \kappa_{R_2}\,(1.0 - \text{SOH})], &
Q_{\text{act}}(T, \text{SOH}) &= \text{SOH} \cdot Q_{\text{init}} f_Q(T), \label{eqn:aging_q}
\end{align}
where $\kappa_{R_i}$ are resistance degradation factors and $f_Q(T)$ captures sub-ambient capacity loss via activation energy $E_{a,Q}$ as well as warm recovery via coefficient $\kappa_{T,\text{hot}}$. Concurrently, double-layer capacitances $C_i$ decay linearly with $(1-\text{SOH})$ at rates $\gamma_{C,i}$ (calibrated constants appear in Section~\ref{sec:exp_setup} and Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}).

Crucially, we enforce a strict structural parameter boundary separating the aging plant from all estimators. Plant parameters evolve with SOH and temperature, whereas fixed-prior observers (EKF, AEKF, UKF, SMO) keep fresh nominal priors ($100\%$ SOH, $25^\circ\text{C}$) without oracle leakage. Because a single terminal-voltage sensor cannot uniquely separate $R_0$, $R_1$, and $R_2$ online without collinear drift, DEKF adapts only the orthogonal parameter estimate vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k = [\hat{R}_{0,k}, 1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}]^T$ (where $\hat{\cdot}$ denotes an estimated quantity) and holds RC branches at nominal priors. At $80\%$ SOH ($\kappa_{R_1}=7.5$), plant $R_1$ grows by $150\%$ ($15.0 \rightarrow 37.5\text{ m}\Omega$ for NMC in Table~\ref{tab:ecm_params}, matching NASA \texttt{B0006} EIS widening $22.37 \rightarrow 38.89\text{ m}\Omega$ in Phase~8). Because $\hat{R}_{0,k}$ alone cannot absorb this unmodeled branch growth, a dynamic polarization voltage residual $\delta V_{\text{pol},k}^*$ remains for PIDKF to compensate (Table~\ref{tab:2_aging}).

\subsection{OCV--SOC Monotonicity and Dynamic Hysteresis}
To preclude spurious local extrema in gradient-based observers, the NMC open-circuit potential $V_{\text{ocv}}(z)$ is calibrated as a strictly monotonic fifth-order polynomial:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:ocv_poly}
V_{\text{ocv}}(z) = p_5 z^5 + p_4 z^4 + p_3 z^3 + p_2 z^2 + p_1 z + p_0,
\end{equation}
whose coefficients $p_0,\dots,p_5$ (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}) yield a fourth-order derivative with no real roots (only complex-conjugate roots with imaginary unit $j = \sqrt{-1}$). This property guarantees a strictly positive slope $V_{\text{ocv}}'(z) \equiv dV_{\text{ocv}}(z)/dz$ bounded below by $\alpha_{\text{NMC}} > 0$ up to maximum slope $V'_{\max}$, ensuring global invertibility via the Inverse Function Theorem. For this three-state system, the discrete observability matrix $\mathcal{O}_3 \equiv \mathcal{O}_k$ admits the Vandermonde determinant (where $\det(\cdot)$ denotes matrix determinant):
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:det_obs}
\det(\mathcal{O}_k) = V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k) (1 - a_1)(1 - a_2)(a_2 - a_1),
\end{equation}
where $V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k) \equiv dV_{\text{ocv}}/dz|_{z_k}$ is the local OCV slope and $a_i \equiv e^{-\Delta t/\tau_i}$ denote distinct RC decay poles. Because $\tau_1 \ne \tau_2$ and $V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k) \ge \alpha_{\text{NMC}} = 0.481\;\mathrm{V}$ across NMC operation, $\mathcal{O}_3$ maintains full rank everywhere ($\kappa(\mathcal{O}_3) = 3.38 \times 10^4$ at $z=0.50$). In contrast, along the LFP plateau, the strictly monotonic smooth two-phase curve
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:ocv_lfp}
V_{\text{ocv}}^{\text{LFP}}(z) = 1.950 + 1.320\,\sigma(26(z - 0.06)) + 0.065\,z + 0.250\,\sigma(24(z - 0.89)),
\end{equation}
with logistic sigmoid function $\sigma(u) = (1 + e^{-u})^{-1}$, lowers the plateau gradient at $z=0.50$ by $11.3$-fold ($0.7434 \rightarrow 0.0659\;\mathrm{V}$ per unit SOC). Consequently, the 3-state Euclidean spectral condition number $\kappa(\mathcal{O}_3) \equiv \|\mathcal{O}_3\|_2 \|\mathcal{O}_3^{-1}\|_2$ (where $\|\cdot\|_2$ denotes the induced spectral norm) jumps by over an order of magnitude to $4.86 \times 10^5$. For the 4-state hysteresis-augmented observability matrix $\mathcal{O}_4 \equiv \mathcal{O}_{4,k}$ derived below, it reaches $\kappa(\mathcal{O}_4) = 1.20 \times 10^8$, exposing why tiny millivolt errors amplify into large SOC shifts along flat plateaus.

Physically, LFP undergoes a two-phase transition ($\text{LiFePO}_4 \leftrightarrow \text{FePO}_4$) that, under the Gibbs phase rule at fixed temperature and pressure, pins lithium chemical potential across intermediate stoichiometries. This phase coexistence flattens the equilibrium curve (median plateau slope $0.0837\;\mathrm{V}$ across $z\in[0.20,0.80]$, floor $\alpha_{\text{LFP}} = 0.0659\;\mathrm{V}$) and generates pronounced path-dependent hysteresis~\cite{Roscher2011hysteresis,Verbrugge2004hysteresis}. We represent this dynamic hysteresis overpotential $V_h(t)$ using a single-state decay model parameterized by dimensionless rate $\beta = 45.0$ and plateau envelope $M(z) \equiv M_0 = 30.0\;\mathrm{mV}$ (bounded by $M_{\max}$):
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:hysteresis_diff}
\frac{d V_h(t)}{dt} = -\frac{\beta \eta |I(t)|}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}(T, \text{SOH})} \left[ V_h(t) - M(z) \operatorname{sgn}(I(t)) \right],
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sgn}(\cdot)$ is the signum function. Exact ZOH integration over one sampling step with per-step SOC increment $|\Delta z_k| \equiv |z_{k+1} - z_k|$ (where $z_k \equiv \text{SOC}_k \in [0, 1]$) yields a contractive convex update governed by hysteresis decay factor $a_{h,k} \equiv e^{-\beta |\Delta z_k|}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:hysteresis_discrete}
V_{h,k+1} = a_{h,k} V_{h,k} + \left(1 - a_{h,k}\right) M(z_k) \operatorname{sgn}(I_k),
\end{equation}
which guarantees that $V_{h,k}$ remains positively invariant within $[-M_{\max}, M_{\max}]$. Augmenting the LFP state vector to $\mathbf{x}_k^{\text{LFP}} = [\text{SOC}_k,\, U_{1,k},\, U_{2,k},\, V_{h,k}]^T$ gives the four-state discrete system:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:x_lfp}
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}^{\text{LFP}} = \mathbf{A}_k^{\text{LFP}} \mathbf{x}_k^{\text{LFP}} + \mathbf{B}_k^{\text{LFP}} I_k + \mathbf{g}_k + \mathbf{w}_k,
\end{equation}
where $[B_{1,k}, B_{2,k}, B_{3,k}]^T = \mathbf{B}_k$ from Eq.~\eqref{eqn:Bk_mat}, and the augmented state-transition matrix $\mathbf{A}_k^{\text{LFP}}$, input vector $\mathbf{B}_k^{\text{LFP}}$, and nonlinear hysteresis excitation vector $\mathbf{g}_k$ are:
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{A}_k^{\text{LFP}} &= \operatorname{diag}(1, a_1, a_2, a_{h,k}), \qquad
\mathbf{B}_k^{\text{LFP}} = [B_{1,k},\, B_{2,k},\, B_{3,k},\, 0]^T, \\
\mathbf{g}_k &= \left[0,\, 0,\, 0,\, (1 - a_{h,k}) M(z_k) \operatorname{sgn}(I_k)\right]^T.
\end{align*}
Terminal voltage measurement becomes:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:vt_lfp}
y_k = V_k = V_{\text{ocv}}(\text{SOC}_k) - V_{h,k} - U_{1,k} - U_{2,k} - I_k R_0 + v_k,
\end{equation}
and over short frozen-coefficient windows ($a_h \equiv a_{h,k}$), the augmented local observability determinant expands to:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:det_obs_lfp}
|\det(\mathcal{O}_{4,k})| = V_{\text{ocv}}'(z_k)(1 - a_h)|a_1 - a_2|\prod_{i=1}^2 (1 - a_i)|a_i - a_h|.
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eqn:det_obs_lfp} exposes a structural dichotomy between rest and dynamic excitation. During open-circuit rest ($|I_k| < 10\;\mathrm{mA}$), $a_{h,k} = 1$ becomes collinear with the SOC integrator mode, so state and parameter Kalman gain vectors are frozen ($\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} = \mathbf{0}$, Section~3). Under Persistent Excitation (PE, non-vanishing window-averaged current), $a_{h,k} < 1$ separates from unity and from both RC poles except at isolated discrete resonance currents $\mathcal{I}_{\text{res}}$ of zero Lebesgue measure ($\mu_L(\mathcal{I}_{\text{res}}) = 0$).

Despite these large static condition numbers, persistent dynamic current excitation preserves local observability. Using state coordinates normalized by their characteristic voltage scales, $\tilde{\mathbf{x}} = [z, U_1/0.1, U_2/0.1, V_h/0.03]^T$, we evaluate the 4-state LTV observability Gramian $\mathcal{G}_o$ across $9,759$ active $30\;\mathrm{s}$ windows with root-mean-square (RMS) current $I_{\text{RMS}} \ge 0.5\;\mathrm{A}$ at fresh $25^\circ\text{C}$ operation. These comprise window counts $M_w$ of $3,506$ UDDS, $571$ US06, $3,554$ FUDS, and $2,128$ DST windows ($29,277$ total across $80\%\text{--}100\%$ SOH). Across every active window, $\mathcal{G}_o$ remains strictly positive-definite, with minimum eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{G}_o) \ge 1.09 \times 10^{-7}$ on UDDS, $\ge 1.14 \times 10^{-7}$ on US06, $\ge 9.45 \times 10^{-8}$ on FUDS, and $\ge 7.15 \times 10^{-10}$ on DST. Across $80\%\text{--}100\%$ SOH on UDDS, $\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{G}_o)$ likewise stays $\ge 5.10 \times 10^{-8}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$, $\ge 3.46 \times 10^{-8}$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$, and $\ge 7.79 \times 10^{-8}$ at $45^\circ\text{C}$.

Physically, dynamic hysteresis tracking improves LFP precision through \emph{systematic bias cancellation} rather than improved matrix conditioning. Absorbing the $\pm 30\;\mathrm{mV}$ ($M_0 = 30\;\mathrm{mV}$) charge--discharge gap into $V_{h,k}$ eliminates persistent voltage innovation bias, so the weak OCV slope no longer fights unmodeled hysteresis residuals (Fig.~\ref{fig:0_mechanism}).

\section{State-of-Charge Estimation Methodologies}
We group the nine evaluated estimators into three complementary paradigms within a unified discrete-time notation: (i)~recursive model-based observers spanning Taylor-linearized, sigma-point, covariance-adaptive, dual state--parameter, and sliding-mode filtering (EKF, UKF, AEKF, DEKF, and SMO; Sections~3.1--3.4), (ii)~standalone sequence and physics-informed neural networks (BiLSTM-Attn, Unconstrained MLP, and PINN-SOC; Sections~3.5--3.6), and (iii)~the proposed hybrid PIDKF (Section~3.7).

\subsection{Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) and Sigma-Point Variant (UKF)}
Within the nonlinear Kalman family, UKF~\cite{Plett2006ukf,He2013ukf} propagates $2n+1$ deterministic sigma points over state dimension $n \in \{3,4\}$. Using identical noise covariances ($\mathbf{Q}_k \equiv \mathbf{Q}, R_v$) and scaled unscented transform (UT) parameters ($\alpha_{\text{UT}} = 10^{-3}$, $\beta_{\text{UT}} = 2$, $\kappa_{\text{UT}} = 0$), UKF matches EKF precision closely while requiring triple the MCU latency ($12.98\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$ vs.\ $3.69\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$, Table~\ref{tab:8_embedded}).

At each step $k$, EKF linearizes terminal voltage observation around the \emph{a priori} state estimate $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-$ (with \emph{a priori} SOC component $\hat{z}_k^-$) via Jacobian $\mathbf{H}_k$, where superscripts $-$ and $+$ denote \emph{a priori} and \emph{a posteriori} estimates~\cite{Plett2004part3}. The filter then alternates between time prediction and Joseph-stabilized measurement correction:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{H}_k^{\text{NMC}} &= \begin{bmatrix} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^-) & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\mathbf{H}_k^{\text{LFP}} = \begin{bmatrix} V_{\text{ocv}}'(\hat{z}_k^-) & -1 & -1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}, \label{eqn:ekf_jacobian}\\
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^- &= \mathbf{A}_{k-1} \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}^+ + \mathbf{B}_{k-1} I_{k-1} + \mathbf{g}_{k-1}, \qquad
\mathbf{P}_k^- = \mathbf{A}_{k-1} \mathbf{P}_{k-1}^+ \mathbf{A}_{k-1}^T + \mathbf{Q}, \label{eqn:ekf_pred_cov}\\
e_k &= V_k - \hat{V}_{t,k}^-, \qquad \mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T \left( \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T + R_v \right)^{-1}, \label{eqn:ekf_gain}\\
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^+ &= \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^- + \mathbf{K}_k e_k, \qquad
\mathbf{P}_k^+ = (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k) \mathbf{P}_k^- (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k \mathbf{H}_k)^T + \mathbf{K}_k R_v \mathbf{K}_k^T, \label{eqn:ekf_joseph}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{P}_k^\pm$ are state covariances, $\mathbf{g}_{k-1}$ vanishes for NMC and follows Eq.~\eqref{eqn:hysteresis_discrete} for LFP, $e_k$ is voltage innovation relative to $\hat{V}_{t,k}^-$, and $\mathbf{K}_k$ is the Kalman gain with identity matrix $\mathbf{I}$, preserving symmetry and positive-definiteness ($\mathbf{P}_k^+ \succ 0$ in the Loewner ordering).

\subsection{Adaptive Extended Kalman Filter (AEKF)}
To adapt measurement noise covariance under regime shifts, AEKF augments EKF with a Sage--Husa (SH) online estimator~\cite{Xiang2022aekf}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:sh_meas_cov}
\hat{r}_k = (1 - d_k)\hat{r}_{k-1} + d_k \left( V_k - \hat{V}_{t,k}^- \right), \qquad
\hat{R}_{v,k} = (1 - d_k)\hat{R}_{v,k-1} + d_k \left( e_k^2 - \mathbf{H}_k \mathbf{P}_k^- \mathbf{H}_k^T \right),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{r}_k$ is estimated innovation bias, $\hat{R}_{v,k}$ is adaptive scalar measurement noise variance projected onto a lower floor $R_{v,\min} > 0$ ($\mathbf{Q}$ held fixed for unique identifiability), and $d_k$ is an exponential weighting sequence governed by forgetting factor $\lambda_{\text{SH}}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}).

\subsection{Dual Extended Kalman Filter (DEKF)}
To separate fast state dynamics from slow degradation, DEKF couples a fast state filter with a slow random-walk parameter filter $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{k+1} = \boldsymbol{\theta}_k + \mathbf{w}_{\theta,k}$ ($\mathbf{w}_{\theta,k} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}_\theta)$, tracking $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k = [\hat{R}_{0,k}, 1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}]^T$). Because parameter updates also perturb future internal states, total sensitivity propagates via Plett's total derivative recursion~\cite{Plett2004part3}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:dekf_param_jacobian}
\mathbf{C}_{\theta,k} = \left.\frac{\partial g(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-, I_k, \boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}}\right|_{\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k^-} + \mathbf{H}_k \frac{d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-}{d\boldsymbol{\theta}}, \qquad
\frac{d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-}{d\boldsymbol{\theta}} = \mathbf{A}_{k-1} \frac{d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}^+}{d\boldsymbol{\theta}} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{f}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\theta}},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}$ is the parameter observation Jacobian and $g(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-, I_k, \boldsymbol{\theta}) \equiv \hat{V}_{t,k}^-$ is the voltage observation function ($\partial g/\partial\boldsymbol{\theta} = [-I_k,\, 0]$, since $R_{0,k}$ acts instantaneously on voltage whereas $1/Q_{\text{act},k}$ enters through the SOC transition). The state-to-parameter derivative $d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-/d\boldsymbol{\theta}$ propagates from $d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}^+/d\boldsymbol{\theta} = d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}^-/d\boldsymbol{\theta} - \mathbf{K}_{k-1}\mathbf{C}_{\theta,k-1}$, and $\partial \mathbf{f}/\partial \boldsymbol{\theta} = [0,\, -\eta\Delta t I_k/3600;\, 0,\, 0;\, 0,\, 0]$ (appending $[0,\, 0]$ for LFP) is the partial derivative of discrete transition $\mathbf{f}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k-1}^+, I_{k-1}, \boldsymbol{\theta})$. Using parameter innovation variance $R_{\theta,v}$, covariances $\mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^\pm$, and rest gating ($\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} = \mathbf{0}$ for $|I_k| < 10\;\mathrm{mA}$), Joseph-stabilized parameter updates follow:
\begin{align}\label{eqn:dekf_param_cov}
\mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} &= \mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^- \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}^T [ \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k} \mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^- \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}^T + R_{\theta,v} ]^{-1}, \qquad
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k^+ = \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k^- + \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} e_k, \nonumber\\
\mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^+ &= (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}) \mathbf{P}_{\theta,k}^- (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k})^T + \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} R_{\theta,v} \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k}^T.
\end{align}

\subsection{Sliding Mode Observer (SMO)}
The SMO enforces robustness against bounded model-plant mismatch by driving voltage tracking errors onto a boundary layer of thickness $\mu$ around the zero-voltage-error sliding manifold $S_k = V_{\text{meas},k} - \hat{V}_{t,k} = 0$. Here, $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k$ denotes the estimated state vector (with SOC coordinate $\hat{z}_k$, abbreviated $\hat{z}$), $V_{\text{meas},k} \equiv V_k$ is measured terminal voltage, and $\hat{V}_{t,k}$ is the observer voltage prediction~\cite{Chen2016smo,Huangfu2018smo}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:smo_state_eq}
\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{k+1} = \mathbf{A}_k \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k + \mathbf{B}_k I_k + \mathbf{g}_k + \mathbf{L}(\hat{z}_k) \operatorname{sat}\left( \frac{S_k}{\mu} \right),
\end{equation}
with continuous saturation function $\operatorname{sat}(\cdot)$ and scheduled state-feedback injection vector $\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}_k)$. To prevent gain explosion across flat plateaus while preserving reachability on steep OCV slopes, switching injection acts exclusively along the SOC coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}) = \left[\frac{l_{\text{smo}}}{\max(\alpha_{\min}, V_{\text{ocv}}^\prime(\hat{z}))},\, 0,\, 0,\, (0)\right]^T,
\end{equation}
where $l_{\text{smo}}$ is the scalar injection gain, $\alpha_{\min}$ is the minimum derivative regularization threshold, and the parenthesized fourth entry applies to the four-state LFP model (Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}). Whenever $V_{\text{ocv}}^\prime(\hat{z}) \ge \alpha_{\min}$, the effective manifold gain $\gamma(\hat{z}) \equiv \mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}) = l_{\text{smo}}$ strictly exceeds the nominal disturbance bound $\Phi_d$ (covering worst-case unmodeled branch overpotential mismatch from current bias and resistance drift). Meanwhile, setting the polarization and hysteresis coordinates of $\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}_k)$ to zero ($l_2 = l_3 = l_h = 0$) lets internal overpotentials evolve along passive dissipative dynamics without high-frequency switching chattering (discrete quasi-sliding mode proof in Appendix~\ref{app:lyapunov}).

\subsection{Bidirectional LSTM with Self-Attention (BiLSTM-Attn)}
BiLSTM-Attn ingests a causal sliding telemetry window $\mathbf{X}_k = [I_{k-W+1:k},\, V_{k-W+1:k},\, T_{k-W+1:k}] \in \mathbb{R}^{W \times 3}$ of look-back length $W = 30\;\mathrm{s}$ in real space $\mathbb{R}^{W \times 3}$. Strictly within each past $W$-step window, recurrent layers extract forward ($\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t$) and backward ($\overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t$) hidden states of dimension $D = 32$ per direction ($13,698$ \texttt{float32} parameters; $53.51\;\mathrm{KB}$ weights Flash)~\cite{Song2019bilstm,Vaswani2017attention}. Concatenating both directions (denoted $\|$) as $\mathbf{h}_t = [\overrightarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t \,\|\, \overleftarrow{\mathbf{h}}_t] \in \mathbb{R}^{2 D}$ at step $t \in \{1,\dots,W\}$, self-attention forms alignment scores $u_t$, weights $\alpha_t$, and context vector $\mathbf{c}_k$ via learnable matrix $\mathbf{W}_a$, bias $\mathbf{b}_a$, and vector $\mathbf{v}$ before linear decoding into $\hat{z}_k$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:attn_context}
u_t = \mathbf{v}^T \tanh(\mathbf{W}_a \mathbf{h}_t + \mathbf{b}_a), \qquad
\alpha_t = \frac{\exp(u_t)}{\sum_{j=1}^W \exp(u_j)}, \qquad
\mathbf{c}_k = \sum_{t=1}^W \alpha_t \mathbf{h}_t.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN-SOC)}
Rather than treating SOC estimation as unconstrained regression, PINN-SOC embeds physical conservation laws directly into the training graph~\cite{Raissi2019pinn,Brancato2026}. To preserve strict time-shift invariance over multi-hour duty cycles, the neural-network operator $\mathcal{NN}_1$ ingests strictly causal telemetry $\mathbf{u}_k = [I_k, V_{t,k}, T_k, dV_{t,k}/dt, \hat{\text{SOH}}_{\text{latched}}]^T \in \mathbb{R}^5$ with voltage $V_{t,k} \equiv V_k$ and temperature $T_k$, omitting raw clock time $t_k$. During blind test evaluation, latched health indicator $\hat{\text{SOH}}_{\text{latched}}$ remains at nominal $1.00$ without oracle aging leakage~\cite{Le2026charging}. Charge depletion obeys the Faradaic conservation ordinary differential equation (ODE):
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:pinn_ode}
\frac{d\,\text{SOC}(t)}{dt} = -\frac{\eta(I)\, I(t)}{3600\,Q_{\text{act}}(T, \text{SOH})}.
\end{equation}
Rather than explicit dependence on raw clock time, discrete sample differences yield $d\hat{z}/dt \approx (\hat{z}_{k+1} - \hat{z}_k)/\Delta t$ for the Faradaic residual $\mathcal{R}_{\text{Faraday},k}$, while automatic differentiation with respect to terminal voltage $\partial \hat{z}/\partial V_{t,k}$ enforces thermodynamic monotonicity:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{R}_{\text{Faraday},k} &= \frac{\hat{z}_{k+1} - \hat{z}_k}{\Delta t} + \frac{\eta(I_k)\, I_k}{3600\,Q_{\text{nom}} \hat{\text{SOH}}_{\text{latched},k}}, \label{eqn:res_faraday}
\end{align}
where $Q_{\text{nom}} \equiv Q_{\text{init}}$ is the nominal fresh capacity and online inference remains strictly causal at each $1\;\mathrm{s}$ step.

Simultaneously, because internal polarization and hysteresis voltages ($\hat{V}_{\text{pol}} \equiv \hat{U}_1 + \hat{U}_2 + \hat{V}_h$) cannot be measured directly in field packs, PINN-SOC enforces terminal-voltage Kirchhoff consistency across the residual manifold:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:res_kvl}
\mathcal{R}_{\text{KVL}}(t) = V_{\text{meas}}(t) - \left[ V_{\text{ocv}}(\hat{z}(t)) - I(t) R_0 - \hat{V}_{\text{pol}}(t) \right],
\end{equation}
while an output Sigmoid activation guarantees bounded estimates $\hat{z} \in (0, 1)$. During offline optimization over parameters $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{PINN}}$ ($6,690$ \texttt{float32} weights, $26.13\;\mathrm{KB}$ Flash), total objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}$ balances supervised data fidelity $\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}$ against physics losses ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{Faraday}}, \mathcal{L}_{\text{KVL}}, \mathcal{L}_{\text{mono}}$) weighted by $\lambda_{\text{Faraday}}, \lambda_{\text{KVL}}, \lambda_{\text{mono}}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:pinn_loss}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{PINN}}) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} + \lambda_{\text{Faraday}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Faraday}} + \lambda_{\text{KVL}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{KVL}} + \lambda_{\text{mono}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{mono}},
\end{equation}
where individual terms penalize prediction errors, charge-conservation violations, voltage residuals, and negative OCV--SOC monotonicity ($\partial \hat{z}/\partial V_t \ge 0$, ensuring SOC rises with voltage):
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} &= \frac{1}{N_d} \sum_{k=1}^{N_d} (\hat{z}_k - \text{SOC}_k^{\text{ref}})^2, \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{Faraday}} = \frac{1}{N_c} \sum_{j=1}^{N_c} \|\mathcal{R}_{\text{Faraday}}(t_j)\|^2, \label{eqn:pinn_loss_data_faraday} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{KVL}} &= \frac{1}{N_c} \sum_{j=1}^{N_c} \|\mathcal{R}_{\text{KVL}}(t_j)\|^2, \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{mono}} = \frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{j=1}^{N_c} \left[\max\left(0, -\frac{\partial \hat{z}_j}{\partial V_{t,j}}\right)\right]^2, \label{eqn:pinn_loss_kvl_mono}
\end{align}
evaluated over $N_d$ labeled samples (reference state $\text{SOC}_k^{\text{ref}}$) and $N_c$ collocation timestamps $t_j$ ($j \in \{1,\dots,N_c\}$, with estimates $\hat{z}_j \equiv \hat{z}(t_j)$ and voltages $V_{t,j} \equiv V_t(t_j)$). PINN-SOC trains as a four-layer MLP with Gaussian Error Linear Unit (GELU) activations using decoupled weight-decay Adaptive Moment Estimation (AdamW; hyperparameters in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}). Under blind fresh-prior evaluation without online parameter adaptation, however, soft KVL and Faraday penalties stiffen fixed-parameter errors compared to an unconstrained MLP ($3.625\pm0.851\%$ vs.\ $3.201\pm0.518\%$, Table~\ref{tab:1_udds}), directly motivating recursive state--parameter fusion in PIDKF.

\subsection{Proposed Hybrid Architecture: PIDKF}\label{sec:pidkf}
\emph{Backbone, Resistance Conditioning, and Passivity Saturation.} To unite the recursive stability and online parameter tracking of DEKF with physics-constrained neural compensation, we introduce the hybrid \textbf{PIDKF} (Fig.~\ref{fig:0_framework}). Its DEKF backbone tracks state $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k$ (3-state for NMC and 4-state including dynamic hysteresis $\hat{V}_{h,k}$ via Eq.~\eqref{eqn:vt_lfp} for LFP) alongside parameters $\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k = [\hat{R}_{0,k}, 1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}]^T$, initialized strictly from fresh nominal priors ($100\%$ SOH, $25^\circ\text{C}$). In parallel, a compact neural compensator $\mathcal{NN}(\mathbf{u}_k; \boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}})$ with trainable weight vector $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}}$ predicts unmodeled polarization residuals $\delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}$ from online telemetry and \emph{a priori} states:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:pidkf_input}
\mathbf{u}_k = \left[I_k,\; T_k,\; \hat{z}_k^-,\; \hat{U}_{1,k}^-,\; \hat{U}_{2,k}^-,\; \hat{R}_{0,k}^-\right]^T \in \mathbb{R}^6.
\end{equation}

Conditioning on rapid online series resistance $\hat{R}_{0,k}^-$ (convergence time constant $\tau_R \approx 10\text{--}30\;\mathrm{s}$) rather than slow inverse capacity $1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}$ ($>2,000\;\mathrm{s}$ from uncalibrated fresh priors) avoids oracle health leakage while detecting coupled thermal-aging impedance shifts rapidly. To suppress unphysical predictions, the compensator output is symmetrically clipped to the saturated Butler--Volmer current envelope $V_{\text{pol}}^{\max}(I_k) = \kappa_0 + \kappa_1 \min(|I_k|, I_{\max})$, using $\kappa_0 = 15\;\mathrm{mV}$, $\kappa_1 = 22\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, and $I_{\max} = 6.0\;\mathrm{A}$ ($|V_{\text{pol}}^{\max}| \le 147\;\mathrm{mV}$). A hard dissipative passivity projection then enforces $\delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k} = 0$ whenever $I_k \cdot \delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k} > 0$ outside the $10\;\mathrm{mA}$ rest gate, ensuring that compensated overpotentials always oppose current flow.

\emph{Causal Residual Calibration.} Strict causality separates offline calibration from embedded inference. During offline training on Cell Batch~A DST trajectories, target residuals $\delta V_{t,\text{target},k}$ equal true plant terminal voltage minus fixed-branch 2-RC predictions generated using forward Batch~A ground truth and causal prior estimates, preventing test-set leakage. During online BMS operation, adding $\delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}$ to the ECM voltage prediction yields innovation $e_{k,\text{PIDKF}}$ for state and parameter updates. To maintain balanced Kalman weighting, innovation variances are inflated using neural residual and decoupling standard deviations $\sigma_{\text{NN}} = 8.0\;\mathrm{mV}$ and $\sigma_{\text{approx}} = 2.0\;\mathrm{mV}$. This yields effective variances $R_{\text{eff},k} \equiv R_{\text{eff}} = R_v + \sigma_{\text{NN}}^2 + \sigma_{\text{approx}}^2 = 1.068 \times 10^{-3}\;\mathrm{V}^2$ and $R_{\theta,\text{eff}} = R_{\theta,v} + \sigma_{\text{NN}}^2 = 1.0064 \times 10^{-2}\;\mathrm{V}^2$.

\emph{Exogenous Sensitivity Decoupling.} Differentiating $\delta\hat{V} \equiv \delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}$ through the composite observation model (where $15\;\mathrm{V}^{-1}$ reflects pre-normalization of inputs $\hat{U}_{1,k}^-$ and $\hat{U}_{2,k}^-$) adds neural Jacobian entries $\mathbf{J}_{\text{NN},k}$ to $\mathbf{H}_k$, producing exact Jacobians $\mathbf{H}_{\text{PIDKF},k}^{\text{exact}}$ and $\mathbf{C}_{\theta,\text{PIDKF},k}^{\text{exact}}$:
\begin{align*}
\mathbf{J}_{\text{NN},k} &= \left[\frac{\partial\delta\hat{V}}{\partial\hat{z}_k^-},\; 15\frac{\partial\delta\hat{V}}{\partial\hat{U}_{1,k}^-},\; 15\frac{\partial\delta\hat{V}}{\partial\hat{U}_{2,k}^-},\; (0)\right], \qquad
\mathbf{H}_{\text{PIDKF},k}^{\text{exact}} = \mathbf{H}_k + \mathbf{J}_{\text{NN},k}, \\
\mathbf{C}_{\theta,\text{PIDKF},k}^{\text{exact}} &= \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k} + \mathbf{J}_{\text{NN},k}\frac{d\hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-}{d\boldsymbol{\theta}} + \left[\frac{\partial \delta\hat{V}}{\partial \hat{R}_{0,k}^-},\; 0\right].
\end{align*}
{\sloppy On aged NMC UDDS operation (Phase~10), automatic differentiation shows $\|\mathbf{J}_{\text{NN},k}\|_2 / \|\mathbf{H}_k\|_2$ averages $16.8\%$ (peak $28.7\%$) and reverses sign near clamping knees. Feeding these noisy derivatives directly into Kalman gain $\mathbf{K}_k$ destabilizes covariance contraction, raising error to $1.894\pm0.495\%$ (NMC) and $1.033\pm0.468\%$ (LFP) at $85\%$ SOH. PIDKF therefore applies \emph{exogenous sensitivity decoupling} (setting decoupled PIDKF observation Jacobians to $\mathbf{H}_{\text{PIDKF},k} \approx \mathbf{H}_k$ and $\mathbf{C}_{\theta,\text{PIDKF},k} \approx \mathbf{C}_{\theta,k}$) while absorbing the bounded mismatch in $\sigma_{\text{approx}}^2$. This choice slashes aged $85\%$ SOH RMSE to $0.554\pm0.018\%$ (NMC) and $0.593\pm0.048\%$ (LFP) and eliminates neural backpropagation on the MCU.\par}

\emph{Physics-Constrained Training Objective.} The compensator acts as a Butler--Volmer-constrained overpotential surrogate trained over $N$ samples via objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{PIDKF}}$, combining residual error $\mathcal{L}_{\text{res}} = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N (\delta\hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k} - \delta V_{t,\text{target},k})^2$, physics penalty $\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}}$ (weight $\lambda_{\text{phys}} = 0.50$, passivity multiplier $\gamma_I = 1.0$), and $L_2$ weight decay ($\lambda_{\text{reg}} = 10^{-5}$):
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{PIDKF}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}}) &= \mathcal{L}_{\text{res}} + \lambda_{\text{phys}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} + \lambda_{\text{reg}} \|\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\text{net}}\|_2^2, \label{eqn:loss_pidkf}\\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N \left[\max\left(0, |\delta \hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}| - V_{\text{pol}}^{\max}\right)^2 + \gamma_I \max\left(0, I_k \delta \hat{V}_{t,\text{comp},k}\right)^2\right]. \label{eqn:loss_pidkf_phys}
\end{align}
The trained network forms a compact $[6 \rightarrow 16 \rightarrow 8 \rightarrow 1]$ GELU MLP with $257$ \texttt{float32} weights ($1.00\;\mathrm{KB} = 1,028\;\mathrm{B}$ Flash), requiring $580$ compensator floating-point operations (FLOPs) per step ($1,540$ total PIDKF FLOPs/step, Table~\ref{tab:8_embedded}).

\emph{Closed-Loop ISS and UUB Stability.} Closed-loop ISS and UUB convergence follow physically from three safeguards formalized in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}: (i)~hard Butler--Volmer clamping bounds total unmodeled voltage perturbation by $\bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{tot}}$, (ii)~rest-period gain freezing keeps error dynamics non-expansive during idle pauses, and (iii)~active-window observability ensures periodic state-covariance contraction~\cite{Reif1999ekf}. Under assumptions (A1)--(A4) in Appendix~\ref{app:parameters}, the \emph{a priori} state error $\mathbf{e}_k^- \equiv \mathbf{x}_k - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_k^-$ therefore satisfies uniform first-moment and mean-square UUB bounds:
\begin{align}
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \|\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{e}_k^-]\| &\le \frac{c \bar{A} \bar{K} \bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{tot}}}{1 - \rho} < \infty, \label{eqn:bibs_pidkf}\\
\limsup_{k \to \infty} \mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{e}_k^-\|^2] &\le n\bar{p} + 2\sqrt{n\bar{p}}\left(\frac{c \bar{A} \bar{K} \bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{tot}}}{1 - \rho}\right) + \left(\frac{c \bar{A} \bar{K} \bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{tot}}}{1 - \rho}\right)^2 < \infty, \label{eqn:ms_pidkf}
\end{align}
where $\mathbb{E}[\cdot]$ denotes mathematical expectation, $\limsup$ is the limit superior, $n \in \{3, 4\}$ is state dimension, $\bar{p}$ is the upper Riccati covariance bound ($\underline{p}\mathbf{I} \preceq \mathbf{P}_k^- \preceq \bar{p}\mathbf{I}$ under Loewner inequality $\preceq$). Constants $\bar{A}$ and $\bar{K}$ are uniform spectral bounds on $\|\mathbf{A}_k\|_2$ and $\|\mathbf{K}_k\|_2$, $c \ge 1$ is the transition prefactor, and $\rho < 1$ is the geometric multi-cycle error contraction rate.

\section{Experimental Benchmarking Platform and Methodology\label{sec:exp_setup}}
To prevent data leakage, every experiment separates development trajectories (Batch~A, used strictly for 2-RC parameter identification and neural training) from held-out evaluation trajectories (Batch~B, tested blindly from $100\%$ SOH, $25^\circ\text{C}$ priors) across two complementary tiers:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Tier~1 Public Laboratory Cycler Validation:} Direct testing uses held-out cycler records from two open repositories. First, \textbf{XJTU Batch-5}~\cite{Wang2024xjtu} provides $1\text{--}3\text{C}$ charge and dynamic RW pulses ($-2\text{ A}$ to $-7\text{ A}$) on $2.00\;\mathrm{Ah}$ LISHEN $\text{LiNi}_{0.5}\text{Co}_{0.2}\text{Mn}_{0.3}\text{O}_2$ (NMC532) $18650$ cells. Cells \texttt{RW\_battery-1} and \texttt{RW\_battery-2} form Batch~A for 2-RC calibration ($R_0 = 32.0\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $R_1 = 7.8\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $R_2 = 11.0\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $39.45\;\mathrm{mV}$ fit RMS), with capacity $Q_{\text{init}} = 1.865\;\mathrm{Ah}$ measured from a $1\text{C}$ reference performance test (RPT), and provide neural training data. Held-out Batch~B cells \texttt{RW\_battery-3} and \texttt{RW\_battery-4} are tested fresh ($100\%$) and aged ($\sim 86.0\text{--}86.8\%$ SOH).

Second, \textbf{NASA Ames PCoE}~\cite{Saha2007nasa} contributes commercial $2.00\;\mathrm{Ah}$ $\text{LiCoO}_2$ $18650$ datasets. Nominal calibration uses \texttt{B0005} cycle~0 ($R_0 = 44.67\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$ from fresh EIS; $15.81\;\mathrm{mV}$ constant-current [CC] RMS), Phase~8 fits $39$-frequency ($0.1\;\mathrm{Hz}\text{--}5\;\mathrm{kHz}$) \texttt{B0006} spectra, and held-out cells \texttt{B0007} ($24^\circ\text{C}$ fresh/$81\%$ aged), \texttt{B0030} ($43^\circ\text{C}$, $4\text{ A}$), and \texttt{B0055/B0056} ($4^\circ\text{C}$, $2\text{ A}$) evaluate thermal and aging transfer (Table~\ref{tab:10_tier1}).
\item \textbf{Tier~2 Calibrated Co-Simulation Testbed:} Literature parameterizations of Samsung INR18650-20R (NMC, $2.00\;\mathrm{Ah}$) and A123 ANR26650M1-B (LFP, $2.30\;\mathrm{Ah}$) dynamics~\cite{He2011ecm,Hu2012ecm,Birkl2017diagnostics,Pecht2011calce} anchor Tier~2 (Table~\ref{tab:ecm_params}) rather than claiming raw Center for Advanced Life Cycle Engineering (CALCE) cycler files. Because physical cells cannot directly expose internal polarization ($U_1, U_2$), hysteresis ($V_h$), or cell-core thermal states without invasive sensors, Phases~1--7 and 9--10 use these calibrated plants to isolate each stressor against exact internal ground truth under strict zero-leakage priors ($100\%$ SOH, $25^\circ\text{C}$). Neural baselines train solely on synthetic Batch~A DST cycles ($100\%, 90\%, 80\%$ SOH) across $5$ seeds ($42\text{--}46$). All estimators are then evaluated on unseen Batch~B UDDS ($3600\text{ s}$ concatenated), FUDS, and $4\text{C}$ US06 ($600\text{ s}$) versus United States Advanced Battery Consortium (USABC) step DST schedules. Performance aggregates report mean plus/minus one standard deviation ($\text{Mean}\pm\text{SD}$), while static zero-heap ANSI-C99 kernels (\texttt{embedded\_c/}) establish ARMv7E-M footprints ($168\;\mathrm{MHz}$ Cortex-M4F and $480\;\mathrm{MHz}$ Cortex-M7; Table~\ref{tab:8_embedded}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Quantitative Performance Indices}
Algorithmic accuracy over $N$ samples relative to $\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}}$ is evaluated using RMSE, mean absolute error (MAE), and maximum absolute error (MaxE). Transient recovery from an initial SOC offset $\Delta\text{SOC}_0 = -40\%$ and lifetime degradation are quantified by first-entry reach time $t_{\text{reach}}^{(2\%)}$ into the $2.0\%$ band, permanent $5.0\%$ settling time $t_{\text{settle}}^{(5\%)}$ through horizon $T_{\text{eval}}$, peak post-reach excursion $\text{MaxE}_{\text{dyn}}$ ($t > t_{\text{reach}}^{(2\%)}$), and the \textbf{Aging Sensitivity Factor} ($\text{ASF}$):
\begin{align}
\text{RMSE} &= \sqrt{\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^N (\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_k)^2} \times 100\%, &
\text{MAE} &= \tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{k=1}^N |\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_k| \times 100\%, \label{eqn:mae}\\
\text{MaxE} &= \max_{1 \le k \le N} |\text{SOC}_k^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_k| \times 100\%, &
t_{\text{reach}}^{(2\%)} &= \min\{ t \ge 0 \mid |\text{SOC}_t^{\text{true}} - \hat{z}_t| \le 2.0\% \}, \label{eqn:treach}\\
\text{ASF} &= \frac{\text{RMSE}(\text{SOH}=80\%) - \text{RMSE}(\text{SOH}=100\%)}{\text{RMSE}(\text{SOH}=100\%)} \times 100\%. \label{eqn:asf}
\end{align}

\section{Results and Discussion Across 10 Operational Phases}
We present the comparative results across all ten operational stress phases, organized by physical theme into four blocks: (1)~nominal, aging, and thermal robustness (Phases~1--3), (2)~initialization convergence, multi-attribute trade-offs, and sensor faults (Phases~4, 5, and~7), (3)~flat-plateau LFP observability, real NASA EIS, and 8S pack scaling (Phases~6, 8, and~9), and (4)~analytical embedded sizing, PIDKF ablation, and Tier~1 cycler validation (Phase~10).

\subsection{Nominal Tracking, Progressive Aging, and Thermal Extremes (Phases 1--3)}
\emph{Phase 1 (Nominal $25^\circ\text{C}$ Dynamic Tracking):} Under fresh nominal $25^\circ\text{C}$ UDDS operation ($100\%$ SOH; Table~\ref{tab:1_udds}, Fig.~\ref{fig:udds}), Kalman observers outperform deep baselines by more than an order of magnitude. Fixed-model EKF ($0.067\%$ RMSE), UKF ($0.069\%$), and DEKF ($0.073\%$) achieve top baseline precision, while AEKF records the tightest peak error ($0.432\%$ MaxE, $0.161\%$ RMSE). PIDKF attains $0.258\pm0.111\%$ RMSE ($0.538\%$ MaxE), accepting a minor fresh-state bias from its inflated variance $R_{\text{eff},k}$ in exchange for wide degraded stability. SMO saturates near its $20\text{ mV}$ switching layer ($1.222\%$), whereas standalone neural networks remain at $3.201\text{--}4.250\%$.

\emph{Phase 2 (Progressive Aging from $100\%$ to $80\%$ SOH):} As capacity fades to $80\%$ SOH (Table~\ref{tab:2_aging}, Fig.~\ref{fig:aging}), uncompensated impedance rise triggers severe observer drift. Fixed-prior EKF and UKF suffer \textbf{55.9-fold} and \textbf{54.7-fold} RMSE surges ($0.067\% \rightarrow 3.745\%$ and $0.069\% \rightarrow 3.772\%$), while AEKF rises to $6.020\%$ as unmodeled resistive drops are misattributed to OCV shifts. Co-estimating $\hat{R}_{0,k}$ and $1/\hat{Q}_{\text{act},k}$ cuts aged DEKF error nearly in half ($2.073\%$), though a transient lag persists from $150\%$ $R_1$ growth. \textbf{PIDKF} absorbs this unmodeled polarization mismatch to hold $80\%$ SOH RMSE below $1\%$ ($0.928\pm0.027\%$), whereas standalone neural models show low relative $\text{ASF}$ ($\le 38.6\%$) only because their $100\%$ SOH errors already exceed $3.2\%$.

\emph{Phase 3 (Sub-Zero and Elevated Thermal Extremes):} Under $0^\circ\text{C}$ and $45^\circ\text{C}$ ambient stress starting from $25^\circ\text{C}$ priors (Table~\ref{tab:1_udds}, Fig.~\ref{fig:thermal}), Arrhenius desolvation pushes fixed-prior cold RMSE to $9.566\text{--}10.258\%$. Online $\hat{R}_{0,k}$ tracking reduces DEKF error to $3.806\%$ but leaves frozen RC polarization lag, while standalone neural models show high seed dispersion ($5.972\text{--}8.402\%$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$ and $4.489\text{--}6.853\%$ at $45^\circ\text{C}$). In contrast, \textbf{PIDKF} achieves the highest accuracy at both thermal extremes ($2.526\pm0.098\%$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$ and $0.573\pm0.019\%$ at $45^\circ\text{C}$).

\subsection{Initialization Recovery, Radar Trade-Offs, and Sensor Faults (Phases 4, 5, and 7)}
\emph{Phase 4 (Recovery from Severe Initialization Offset):} Under an abrupt $\Delta\text{SOC}_0 = -40\%$ initialization shock ($\text{SOC}_0^{\text{true}} = 0.85$, $\hat{z}_0 = 0.45$ at $k=0$; Table~\ref{tab:4_conv}, Fig.~\ref{fig:conv}), fixed-prior UKF and EKF enter the $\le 5\%$ band within $7.0\text{--}8.0\text{ s}$ ($\text{MaxE}_{\text{dyn}} = 1.98\text{--}2.02\%$). High-gain SMO settles in $10\text{ s}$ but incurs a $36.95\%$ initial transient spike. Dual filters (DEKF, PIDKF) settle slower ($t_{\text{settle}}^{(5\%)} = 288.0\text{ s}, 336.0\text{ s}$) because the initial voltage shock perturbs $\hat{R}_{0,k}$ until state and parameter covariances decouple, after which both lock onto tight tracking ($\text{MaxE}_{\text{dyn}} = 2.03\text{--}2.05\%$). By contrast, BiLSTM-Attn fails to converge, and memoryless MLPs repeatedly exit $\le 5\%$ ($t_{\text{settle}}^{(5\%)} = 1601.6\text{--}1747.0\text{ s}$).

\emph{Phase 5 (Multi-Attribute Engineering Trade-Offs):} Figure~\ref{fig:radar} aggregates five $1\text{--}10$ normalized criteria: Nominal Accuracy, Aging Robustness, Thermal Robustness, Convergence Speed, and Computational (Embedded) Efficiency. Fixed EKF excels in nominal accuracy ($9.75$), convergence speed ($9.27$), and compute efficiency ($9.08$), yet collapses under aging ($4.01$) and thermal stress ($3.10$). Across the same five axes, DEKF improves overall resilience ($[9.67,$ $6.68,$ $6.97,$ $7.98,$ $8.31]$), while \textbf{PIDKF} spans the widest balanced operating envelope ($[8.57,$ $8.52,$ $8.06,$ $7.76,$ $7.88]$).

\emph{Phase 7 (Current-Sensor Offset and Switching EMI Corruption):} Over a $2$-hour UDDS schedule corrupted by simultaneous $+0.25\text{ A}$ direct-current (DC) Hall bias and $25\text{ mV}$ RMS EMI (Table~\ref{tab:6_sensor}, Fig.~\ref{fig:sensor}), open-loop Coulomb counting drifts linearly to $14.394\%$ RMSE ($24.840\%$ final-60s drift). Switching gain in SMO amplifies inverter ripple to $4.354\%$ RMSE, whereas Kalman observers attenuate high-frequency disturbances cleanly. Benefiting from variance inflation $R_{\text{eff},k}$, \textbf{PIDKF} achieves the lowest RMSE ($1.308\pm0.099\%$) and final drift ($1.237\%$), outperforming EKF ($1.475\%$) and DEKF ($1.579\%$).

\subsection[Flat-Plateau LFP Generalization, Real EIS, and 8S Pack Scaling]{Flat-Plateau $\text{LiFePO}_4$ Generalization, Real EIS, and 8S Pack Scaling (Phases 6, 8, and 9)}
\emph{Phase 6 (Cross-Chemistry Generalization on Flat-Plateau LFP):} Across identical $25^\circ\text{C}$ UDDS and FUDS schedules (Table~\ref{tab:5_lfp}, Fig.~\ref{fig:lfp}), disabling 4-state hysteresis stalls EKF at $0.401\%$ RMSE ($0.698\%$ MaxE) due to the $\pm 30\;\mathrm{mV}$ charge--discharge voltage gap. Enabling dynamic hysteresis bias cancellation reduces EKF and DEKF errors nearly eightfold ($0.049\text{--}0.051\%$ UDDS, $0.050\text{--}0.055\%$ FUDS). Across both NMC and LFP, PIDKF tracks seamlessly ($0.134\pm0.040\%$ LFP UDDS, $0.143\%$ FUDS), outperforming standalone neural models ($1.538\text{--}4.492\%$).

\emph{Phase 8 (Empirical Randles--CPE Impedance Validation on NASA \texttt{B0006}):} To ground our aging resistance laws in experimental electrochemistry, Fig.~\ref{fig:eis} fits the Randles--CPE circuit with series lead inductance $L$ to real $39$-frequency ($0.1\;\mathrm{Hz}\text{--}5\;\mathrm{kHz}$) spectra from NASA Ames PCoE cell \texttt{B0006}:
\begin{equation}\label{eqn:eis_randles}
Z(j\omega) = j\omega L + R_0 + \frac{R_{\text{ct}} + Z_w(j\omega)}{1 + (j\omega)^\alpha Q_{\text{cpe}} \left(R_{\text{ct}} + Z_w(j\omega)\right)}, \qquad Z_w(j\omega) = \sigma_w \omega^{-1/2}(1 - j),
\end{equation}
where $Z(j\omega)$ is complex impedance at angular frequency $\omega$ ($\mathrm{rad}\cdot\mathrm{s}^{-1}$), $Z_w(j\omega)$ is Warburg diffusion impedance, $R_0 = R_{\text{ohm}} + R_{\text{sei}}$, $R_{\text{ct}}$ is charge-transfer resistance, $Q_{\text{cpe}}$ ($\mathrm{mF}\cdot\mathrm{s}^{\alpha-1}$) and $\alpha$ define the CPE dispersion element, and $\sigma_w$ ($\mathrm{m}\Omega\cdot\mathrm{s}^{-1/2}$) is the Warburg coefficient. Complex least-squares fitting achieves $<2.8\%$ RMS fit error across all three degradation stages:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Fresh ($\sim 100\%$ SOH):} $R_0 = 56.67\pm 2.01\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $R_{\text{ct}} = 22.37\pm 2.77\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $L = 0.203\;\upmu\mathrm{H}$, $Q_{\text{cpe}} = 1633.5$, $\alpha = 0.482$, $\sigma_w = 0.29$, RMS $2.54\%$.
\item \textbf{Mid-Life ($\sim 90\%$ SOH):} $R_0 = 65.62\pm 2.30\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $R_{\text{ct}} = 27.19\pm 3.43\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $L = 0.217\;\upmu\mathrm{H}$, $Q_{\text{cpe}} = 1936.3$, $\alpha = 0.454$, $\sigma_w = 0.31$, RMS $2.54\%$.
\item \textbf{End-of-Test ($\sim 75\%$ SOH):} $R_0 = 64.14\pm 4.87\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $R_{\text{ct}} = 38.89\pm 8.04\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$, $L = 0.253\;\upmu\mathrm{H}$, $Q_{\text{cpe}} = 3037.6$, $\alpha = 0.344$, $\sigma_w \approx 0.00$, RMS $2.75\%$.
\end{itemize}
These spectra confirm $+73.8\%$ mid-frequency $R_{\text{ct}}$ semicircle broadening versus only $+13.2\%$ high-frequency alternating-current (AC) $R_0$ growth: double-layer capacitance $C_{\text{dl}}$ shunts $R_{\text{ct}}$ at kilohertz AC excitation, whereas $1\;\mathrm{s}$ BMS sampling experiences this interfacial degradation directly as the $R_1$ polarization surge in Section~2.3.

\emph{Phase 9 (Distributed 8S Series Pack Scaling):} We next test an 8S series pack seeded with cell-to-cell capacity and resistance relative standard deviations ($\sigma_Q = 3.0\%$, $\sigma_R = 10.0\%$)~\cite{Yang2024degradation,Zheng2018inconsistency} (Table~\ref{tab:7_pack}, Fig.~\ref{fig:pack}). Because interior cell~\#4 heats $+2.63^\circ\text{C}$ hotter than perimeter cells, it ages fastest to $Q_{\text{act}} = 1.703\;\mathrm{Ah}$ ($85.0\%$ SOH, $R_0 = 60.5\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$) and drains down to $8.78\%$ SOC at discharge cutoff. A lumped pack observer tracking total string voltage $V_{\text{string}}$ incurs $2.480\%$ mean RMSE and optimistically overestimates cell~\#4 by $+3.303\%$, masking deep over-discharge and risking copper (Cu) dissolution. While distributed DEKF still suffers $2.157\%$ weakest-cell RMSE, \textbf{Distributed PIDKF} confines mean pack error to $0.223\%$ and weakest-cell~\#4 RMSE to $0.544\%$.

\emph{Phase 10B (Five-Seed PIDKF Component Ablation):} Five-seed Tier~2 ablation (Table~\ref{tab:9_pidkf}, Fig.~\ref{fig:pidkf}) isolates three architectural drivers:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exogenous sensitivity decoupling:} Removing noisy neural derivatives from $\mathbf{K}_k$ avoids covariance destabilization, cutting $85\%$ aged NMC error from $1.894\pm0.495\%$ to $0.554\pm0.018\%$ ($7.808\% \rightarrow 5.092\%$ at $0^\circ\text{C}$).

\item \textbf{Isothermal LFP vs.\ cold robustness trade-off:} On isothermal $85\%$ LFP, disabling neural compensation (\texttt{DEKF + Hysteresis}) reaches $0.346\%$ versus $0.593\pm0.048\%$ for Full PIDKF because flat OCV slopes amplify tiny neural-network bias. \end{itemize}

\section{Industrial BMS Implementation Guidelines}
The Pareto frontier across our ten-phase benchmark translates directly into estimator-selection rules for industrial BMS deployments:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Electric vehicle traction packs:} For passenger electric vehicles (EVs) spanning $0^\circ\text{C}\text{--}45^\circ\text{C}$ thermal swings and multi-year aging, \textbf{PIDKF} offers the best accuracy--compute balance ($0.928\pm0.027\%$ at $80\%$ SOH; $0.544\%$ weakest-cell pack RMSE), whereas fixed EKF should be avoided due to $55.9$-fold aging divergence.
\item \textbf{Stationary LFP energy storage:} For isothermal grid-scale LFP systems operating along the flat plateau, \textbf{DEKF with dynamic hysteresis co-estimation} achieves lowest error ($0.051\%$ fresh, $0.346\%$ aged) without auxiliary sensors or neural weights.
\item \textbf{Fault- and EMI-prone drives:} Under $+0.25\;\mathrm{A}$ current bias and $25\;\mathrm{mV}$ EMI ripple, Kalman observers filter high-frequency noise cleanly (PIDKF $1.308\%$, EKF $1.475\%$, DEKF $1.579\%$), whereas SMO amplifies switching chattering ($4.354\%$ RMSE).
\item \textbf{Edge-to-cloud fleet architectures:} Static ANSI-C99 DEKF and PIDKF kernels ($17.74\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$ Cortex-M4F, $4.28\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$ Cortex-M7; $<0.3\%$ CPU load for $16$ cells at $10\;\mathrm{Hz}$) sustain deterministic local safety while offloading fleet prognostics to the cloud.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}
Through a unified dual-tier evaluation spanning Tier~1 public cycler records (XJTU Batch-5, NASA Ames PCoE) and Tier~2 co-simulation across ten phases under blind fresh priors, this benchmark clarifies why room-temperature estimator rankings fail in field BMS deployments. Capacity fade to $80\%$ SOH drives a $55.9$-fold RMSE surge in fixed EKF ($0.067\% \rightarrow 3.745\%$) as $R_{\text{ct}}$ widens ($22.37 \rightarrow 38.89\;\mathrm{m}\Omega$ on NASA \texttt{B0006}), whereas online DEKF tracking cuts aged error to $2.073\%$. Meanwhile, dynamic hysteresis cancellation lowers fresh LFP error nearly eightfold ($0.401\% \rightarrow 0.049\text{--}0.051\%$), and distributed observers remove weakest-cell pack blindness.

Combining DEKF and dynamic hysteresis with fast $\hat{R}_{0,k}^-$ conditioning, exogenous sensitivity decoupling, and Butler--Volmer passivity in \textbf{PIDKF} yields a single chemistry-agnostic estimator. Across unseen stress, PIDKF maintains $0.928\pm0.027\%$ RMSE at $80\%$ SOH, reaches $1.425\text{--}2.750\%$ on held-out XJTU RW tests and $0.636\text{--}1.715\%$ on NASA \texttt{B0007}, and reduces $4^\circ\text{C}$ error by $63\%$, all within $364\;\mathrm{B}$ RAM, $7.25\;\mathrm{KB}$ Flash, and $17.74\;\upmu\mathrm{s}$ per Cortex-M4F step. Future work will run hardware-in-the-loop Data Watchpoint and Trace (DWT) profiling on physical STM32F4/H7 MCUs, extend Tier~1 sub-zero LFP testing, capture $4^\circ\text{C}$ trapped-capacity rebound, and integrate sub-$70\%$ SOH knee-point prognostics.

\section{Discrete Quasi-Sliding Mode Stability and UUB of SMO\label{app:lyapunov}}
Along sliding manifold $S_k = V_{\text{meas},k} - \hat{V}_{t,k}$, subtracting observer voltage prediction from plant terminal voltage gives the one-step manifold error dynamics:
\begin{align*}
S_{k+1} &= S_k + \Delta f_k - \gamma(\hat{z}_k)\operatorname{sat}\!\left(\frac{S_k}{\mu}\right), \\
\Delta f_k &= -\Delta R_0 \Delta I_k + \left[V_{\text{ocv}}'(\xi_{k+1}) - V_{\text{ocv}}'(\xi_k)\right] e_{z,k} - V_{\text{ocv}}'\Delta Q_k \\
&\quad + \sum_{i=1}^2 \left[(1-a_i)e_{U_i,k} - d_{U_i,k}\right] + \left[(1-a_{h,k})e_{h,k} - \Delta M_k\right] + (v_{k+1} - v_k),
\end{align*}
with scheduled gain $\gamma(\hat{z}) \equiv \mathbf{H}_k\mathbf{L}(\hat{z}) = l_{\text{smo}}$ whenever $V_{\text{ocv}}' \ge \alpha_{\min}$, one-step manifold perturbation $\Delta f_k$, ohmic mismatch $\Delta R_0 = R_0^{\text{plant}} - R_0^{\text{nom}}$ across current step $\Delta I_k = I_{k+1} - I_k$, and intermediate OCV points $\xi_k, \xi_{k+1}$. Here, estimation errors are $e_{z,k} \equiv z_k - \hat{z}_k$, $e_{U_i,k} \equiv U_{i,k} - \hat{U}_{i,k}$, and $e_{h,k} \equiv V_{h,k} - \hat{V}_{h,k}$ (abbreviated $e_h$), alongside polarization and hysteresis perturbations $d_{U_i,k}, \Delta M_k$, capacity-mismatch term $V_{\text{ocv}}'\Delta Q_k$, and bounded analog front-end (AFE) noise $|v_k| \le V_{\text{AFE}} = 0.84\text{ mV}$. Choosing quadratic Lyapunov candidate $V_k = \tfrac{1}{2}S_k^2$ yields two properties whenever $|\Delta f_k| \le \Phi_d < l_{\text{smo}} < 2\mu - \Phi_d$ (under nominal tuning $l_{\text{smo}} = 0.025\text{ V}$, $\mu = 0.020\text{ V}$, $\Phi_d = 5.0\text{ mV}$):

\emph{Contraction and Invariance}: Outside boundary layer $\mathcal{B}_\mu \equiv \{|S| \le \mu\}$, minimum step contraction margin $\eta_{\text{reach}} = \min\{l_{\text{smo}} - \Phi_d,\; 2\mu - l_{\text{smo}} - \Phi_d\}$ equals $10.0\text{ mV}$. This margin drives trajectories from initial manifold error $S_0$ at $k=0$ into $\mathcal{B}_\mu$ within $k^* \le \lceil (|S_0| - \mu)/\eta_{\text{reach}} \rceil$ steps (where $\lceil \cdot \rceil$ denotes the ceiling operator). Inside $\mathcal{B}_\mu$, linear contraction factor $|1 - l_{\text{smo}}/\mu| = 0.25 < 1$ makes the smaller band $\Omega_{\text{ult}} \equiv \{|S| \le \Phi_d/(2 - l_{\text{smo}}/\mu) = 6.67\text{ mV}\} \subset \mathcal{B}_\mu$ positively invariant.

\emph{UUB Convergence}: Because polarization and hysteresis gains vanish ($l_2 = l_3 = l_h = 0$), branch errors obey passive ISS bounds $|e_{U_i,k}| \le \bar{e}_U \le 3.5\text{ mV}$ and LFP hysteresis error satisfies $|e_{h,k}| \le \bar{e}_h = 2 M_0 = 60.0\text{ mV}$ (with upper bounds $\bar{e}_U$ and $\bar{e}_h$). By the Mean Value Theorem (MVT) with $|V_{\text{meas}} - \hat{V}_t| \le |S|$ in $\mathcal{B}_\mu$, the unit-consistent SOC bound satisfies $|e_{z,k}| \le (\mu + 2\bar{e}_U + \bar{e}_h + V_{\text{AFE}})/\alpha_{\text{chem}}$ with minimum chemistry OCV slope floor $\alpha_{\text{chem}}$ taking values $\alpha_{\text{NMC}} = 0.4813\;\mathrm{V}$ and $\alpha_{\text{LFP}} = 0.0659\;\mathrm{V}$ per unit SOC. For NMC ($\bar{e}_h = 0$), $|e_z| \le 5.78\%$ across $\mathcal{B}_\mu$ and $\le 3.01\%$ inside $\Omega_{\text{ult}}$. For LFP, finite UUB holds along the plateau for full state error $\mathbf{e}_x$ ($\|\mathbf{e}_x\|_2 < \infty$; formal bound reaches $42.2\%$ in $\mathcal{B}_\mu$ or $22.0\%$ in $\Omega_{\text{ult}}$ when $e_h \to 0$, and saturates physically to $[0, 1]$ without state--hysteresis decoupling).

\noindent\textbf{Discrete-Time Riccati Invariance and Contraction Assumptions:}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item[(A1)] Active drive windows of length $N = 30\text{ s}$ satisfy Uniform Complete Observability (UCO: $\mathcal{G}_o \succeq \alpha_o \mathbf{I}$, lower bound $\alpha_o > 0$) and Uniform Complete Controllability (UCC: uniform process noise bounds $\underline{q}\mathbf{I} \preceq \mathbf{Q}_k \preceq \bar{q}\mathbf{I}$, $\underline{q} > 0$).
\item[(A2)] System norms stay bounded as $\|\mathbf{A}_k\|_2 \le \bar{A}$, observation Jacobian norm $\|\mathbf{H}_k\|_2 \le \bar{H}$, and effective measurement variance bounds $0 < \underline{r} \le R_{\text{eff},k} \le \bar{r}$.
\item[(A3)] Rest intervals $T_{\text{rest},m} \le T_{\max}$ freeze $\mathbf{K}_k = \mathbf{0}, \mathbf{K}_{\theta,k} = \mathbf{0}$, leaving closed-loop transition $\mathbf{F}_k \equiv \mathbf{A}_k(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_k\mathbf{H}_k) = \mathbf{A}_k$ non-expansive ($\|\mathbf{A}_k\|_2 = 1$) with $\le T_{\max}\bar{q}$ covariance growth.
\item[(A4)] Hard overpotential clipping (bound $\bar{V}^*$) and bounded parameter drift $\|\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k\| \le \bar{\theta}$ ($\tilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k \equiv \boldsymbol{\theta}_k - \hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_k$, inducing voltage bound $\bar{\delta}_\theta$) keep step perturbation $|\varepsilon_k| \le \bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{tot}} \equiv \bar{V}^* + \kappa_0 + \kappa_1 I_{\max} + \bar{\delta}_\theta < \infty$.
\end{enumerate}
Let transition product $\boldsymbol{\Phi}_F(k, j) \equiv \prod_{l=j}^{k-1}\mathbf{F}_l$ over index $l$ admit uniform bound $\bar{F} \ge \sup_k \|\mathbf{F}_k\|_2$ (where $\sup$ denotes supremum) and per-cycle active--rest contraction factor $\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}_F(k_m + L_m, k_m)\| \le \rho_{\text{PE}} < 1$ across cycle $m$ (start step $k_m$, duration $L_m \le N + T_{\max}$). Partitioning $[j, k]$ yields uniform exponential contraction and Joseph-stabilized Riccati bounds:
\begin{align*}
\|\boldsymbol{\Phi}_F(k, j)\| &\le c\rho^{k-j}, \qquad
\rho \equiv \rho_{\text{PE}}^{1/(N + T_{\max})} < 1, \qquad
c \equiv \bar{F}^{2(N + T_{\max})} \rho^{-2(N + T_{\max})} \ge 1, \\
\underline{p}\mathbf{I} &\preceq \mathbf{P}_k^- \preceq \bar{p}\mathbf{I}, \quad
\underline{p} = \underline{q}, \quad \bar{p} = (\bar{r}\alpha_o^{-1} + N\bar{q})\bar{A}^{2N} + T_{\max}\bar{q}, \quad
\|\mathbf{K}_k\|_2 \le \frac{\bar{p}\bar{H}}{\underline{r}} \equiv \bar{K} < \infty.
\end{align*}
We finally decompose prior state error $\mathbf{e}_k^- = \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_k + \mathbf{d}_k$ into zero-mean stochastic perturbation $\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_k$ of covariance $\mathbf{P}_k^-$ ($\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_k\|^2] = \operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{P}_k^-) \le n\bar{p}$, where $\operatorname{tr}(\cdot)$ denotes matrix trace) and deterministic bias vector $\mathbf{d}_k$. Bounding $\mathbf{d}_k$ asymptotically by $\bar{d}_\infty$ ($\limsup_{k\to\infty}\|\mathbf{d}_k\| \le \bar{d}_\infty \equiv c\bar{A}\bar{K}\bar{\varepsilon}_{\text{tot}}/(1-\rho)$) recovers the first-moment bound in Eq.~\eqref{eqn:bibs_pidkf}. Expanding $\|\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_k + \mathbf{d}_k\|^2$ and applying the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality ($|\mathbb{E}[\langle \tilde{\mathbf{e}}_k, \mathbf{d}_k \rangle]| \le \sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\|\tilde{\mathbf{e}}_k\|^2]}\|\mathbf{d}_k\|$, where $\langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle$ denotes the Euclidean inner product) then yields the mean-square bound in Eq.~\eqref{eqn:ms_pidkf}.
